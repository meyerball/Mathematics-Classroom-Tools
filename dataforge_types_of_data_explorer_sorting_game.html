The Core Foundation of Statistics & Data Science

All data in the universe boils down to Words vs Numbers.

Before running any graph, machine learning model, or calculation, you must ask: "Is this variable measuring an amount (numbers) or describing a category (labels)?" Let's break down the 4 essential subtypes you will encounter every day.

QUALITATIVE

Categorical Data

Describes characteristics, labels, qualities, or groupings. You cannot calculate a meaningful arithmetic mean directly (e.g. what is the average of Blue and Red?).

1. Nominal (Names) 2. Ordinal (Order)
QUANTITATIVE

Numerical Data

Real counts or measurements where math operations make total sense (e.g. sums, averages, differences). Answers "How many?" or "How much?".

3. Discrete (Counted) 4. Continuous (Measured)

The 4 Subtypes In-Depth (Click any example to inspect)

Categorical

Nominal Data

"Nom" = Name. Categories with NO natural rank or sequence.

The Litmus Test:

Does order matter? No. Is "Blue" greater than "Green"? No, they are just distinct labels.

Real Examples:
Eye Colors Blood Types Postal ZIP codes*

*Notice: ZIP codes use digits, but they are nominal labels!

Categorical

Ordinal Data

"Ord" = Order. Categories with a clear, defined rank, but spacing isn't uniform.

The Litmus Test:

Can you say A > B? Yes. But the gap between 1st and 2nd place may not equal 2nd and 3rd.

Real Examples:
T-Shirt Sizes (S, M, L) Race Medals (Gold/Silver) Customer Satisfaction (1-5★)
Numerical

Discrete Data

Counted Values. Distinct values with gaps in between (usually whole integers).

The Litmus Test:

Can you have 2.37 of these? Usually No! You count them in jumps: 0, 1, 2, 3...

Real Examples:
Number of siblings Cars in a parking lot Website clicks
Numerical

Continuous Data

Measured Values. Any value on a continuum with infinite precision.

The Litmus Test:

Can you add more decimal places with a finer instrument? Yes! (e.g. 72.482 kg).

Real Examples:
Exact runner marathon time Body temperature (°C) Weight of an apple
