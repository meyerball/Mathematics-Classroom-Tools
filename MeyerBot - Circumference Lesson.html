✏️ WRITE THESE KEY NOTES IN THE BLANK LINES ON PAGE 10:

  • Circumference (C): The total perimeter or distance all the way around the outside of a circle.
  • Diameter (d): The straight line passing right through the center from one side to the other.
  • Radius (r): The distance from the center point to the outer edge (Half of the diameter: r = d ÷ 2).
  • Diameter Rule: Diameter is twice the radius: d = 2r.
CORE FORMULAS TO COPY DOWN:
If given Diameter:    C = π × d    (or C = πd)
If given Radius:    C = 2 × π × r    (or C = 2πr)

💡 MeyerBot's Strategy Board: Calculating Circumference

🤖 MeyerBot's Strategy Board

Before touching your calculator, ask yourself:

"Am I given the RADIUS (r) or the DIAMETER (d)?"
  • If given RADIUS (r): The line only goes halfway from the center to edge.
    👉 Use: C = 2 × π × r
  • If given DIAMETER (d): The line goes all the way across through the center.
    👉 Use: C = π × d

Calculator Tip: Use the actual π key on your calculator, and remember to round your answer to 2 decimal places!

m

Step 2: Substitute π = 3.14 and d = 10

Step 3: C = 3.14 × 10

b) Radius = 14 cm, using π = 22/7

cm

Step 1: C = 2 × π × r

Step 2: C = 2 × (22/7) × 14

Step 3: Simplify by dividing 14 by 7: 14 ÷ 7 = 2

Step 4: C = 2 × 22 × 2 = 88

Answer: 88 cm

💡 MeyerBot's Strategy Board: Working Backwards

🤖 MeyerBot's Strategy Board

What if the problem gives you the Circumference (C) and asks for the Diameter or Radius?

To find Diameter:    d = C ÷ π

To find Radius:    r = d ÷ 2    (or r = C ÷ (2 × π))

Calculator Alert: If calculating C ÷ (2 × π), remember to use brackets around the (2 × π) so your calculator divides properly!

Step 6: Problem Solving 1 & 2 (Page 12)

Problem Solving 1: A circle has a circumference of 60 cm

Part a: d = C ÷ π = 60 ÷ π = 19.09859...
Rounding to 1 d.p. gives 19.1 cm

Part b: r = d ÷ 2 = 19.09859... ÷ 2 = 9.54929...
Rounding to 1 d.p. gives 9.5 cm

Problem Solving 2: Car Tyre

A car tyre has a circumference of 120 cm. Which of these is closest to the radius of the tyre?

Options: A) 9.5 cm  |  B) 19.1 cm  |  C) 38.0 cm  |  D) 753.6 cm

Step 1: Radius r = C ÷ (2 × π)

Step 2: r = 120 ÷ (2 × π) = 19.0985... cm

Answer: B (19.1 cm)

💡 MeyerBot's Strategy Board: Rolling Wheel Problems

🤖 MeyerBot's Strategy Board

When a circular wheel rolls along the ground:

Distance in 1 Full Turn = Circumference of the Wheel (C)
Total Distance = Circumference × Number of Turns

Watch the Units Trap! In Problem Solving 3, the diameter is in mm, but the question asks for distance in metres!

  • Convert mm to metres first by dividing by 1000 (530 mm = 0.53 m).

Step 7: Problem Solving 3 - Bicycle Wheel (Page 13)

A wheel on a bicycle has a diameter of 530 mm. The wheel turns 3 times. Approximately how many metres has the bicycle travelled?

Options: 2m  |  5m  |  10m  |  25m

Step 1: Convert units: 530 mm ÷ 1000 = 0.53 m

Step 2: Find circumference for 1 turn: C = π × 0.53 = 1.665 m

Step 3: Multiply by 3 turns: Distance = 1.665 × 3 = 4.995 m

Answer: 4.995 m is closest to 5m
