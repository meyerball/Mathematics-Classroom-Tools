TOPIC 1

Functions & Graphing Techniques

Stage 6 Advanced Syllabus: MA-F1 (Year 11) & MA-F2 (Year 12)

HSC Weighting: ~12% – 16% (12 to 16 Marks)

Syllabus Sub-strands

  • • MA-F1.1: Algebraic properties, absolute values, factor theorem.
  • • MA-F1.2: Linear, quadratic, cubic & polynomial relations.
  • • MA-F1.3: Inverses, piecewise, odd & even functions.
  • • MA-F2: Graph transformations: $y = f(x \pm c)$, $y = af(x)$, $y = f(kx)$, $y = -f(x)$, $y = f(-x)$.

Core Competencies

  • • Finding exact domain and range across non-standard functions.
  • • Algebraic inversion $f^{-1}(x)$ and domain restriction for 1-to-1 bijection.
  • • Solving modulus equations $|f(x)| = g(x)$ algebraically and graphically.
  • • Dilation, reflection, translation sequencing without axis ambiguity.

Examination Role

Functions form the conceptual scaffolding for all calculus topics. Appears in Section I multiple choice and frequently as multi-part modeling or parameter threshold problems in Section II (e.g. Q13–Q15).

Domain, Range & Inverses

• Domain Conditions: Denominator $\neq 0$; under square root $\ge 0$; log arguments $> 0$.
• Inverse Functions: Swap $x$ and $y$ and solve for $y$. Exists if and only if $f(x)$ is one-to-one (passes the Horizontal Line Test). Reflection across $y = x$.
• Rule: $\text{Domain}(f^{-1}) = \text{Range}(f)$ and $\text{Range}(f^{-1}) = \text{Domain}(f)$.

Odd, Even & Symmetries

• Even Function: $f(-x) = f(x)$ for all $x \implies$ symmetric across the $y$-axis (e.g., $x^2$, $\cos x$, $|x|$).
• Odd Function: $f(-x) = -f(x)$ for all $x \implies$ point symmetry of $180^\circ$ around the origin $(0,0)$ (e.g., $x^3$, $\sin x$, $\frac{1}{x}$).
• If $f(-x)$ equals neither, the function has no parity.

Graph Transformation Rules

  • • $y = f(x) + c$: Vertical shift up by $c$ units.
  • • $y = f(x - c)$: Horizontal shift right by $c$ units.
  • • $y = af(x)$: Vertical dilation by factor $a$.
  • • $y = f(kx)$: Horizontal compression by factor $\frac{1}{k}$.
  • • $y = -f(x)$: Reflection across the $x$-axis.
  • • $y = f(-x)$: Reflection across the $y$-axis.

Provided on NESA Reference Sheet

  • $x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$ (Quadratic formula)
  • Distance: $d = \sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$
  • Gradient: $m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} = \tan\theta$
  • Point-gradient: $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$

MUST MEMORISE (Not on Reference Sheet)

  • Formal definitions: Even $f(-x) = f(x)$, Odd $f(-x) = -f(x)$.
  • Domain of inverse: $\text{Dom}(f^{-1}) = \text{Ran}(f)$, $\text{Ran}(f^{-1}) = \text{Dom}(f)$.
  • Perpendicular line condition: $m_1 m_2 = -1 \iff m_2 = -\frac{1}{m_1}$.
  • Absolute value split: $|A| = B \iff A = B$ or $A = -B$ (provided $B \ge 0$).

HSC Marker Insights & Common Pitfalls

  • Restricting Inverse Domains: When inverting parabolas (e.g., $y = x^2 - 4$), students routinely forget to restrict the domain to $x \ge 0$ (or $x \le 0$) to make the function 1-to-1, losing a full mark.
  • Extraneous Modulus Roots: When solving $|ax + b| = cx + d$ by squaring both sides, students often fail to test their roots in the original equation to reject extraneous solutions where $cx + d < 0$.
  • Strict Inequalities in Fractions: For equations like $\frac{1}{x-2} \ge 3$, multiplying by $x-2$ without squaring $(x-2)^2$ drops the negative branch and flips inequality direction illegally.

Question 1.1 • Inverses & Domain Restrictions (Band 5) [4 Marks]

Consider the quadratic function $f(x) = x^2 - 6x + 5$.

  1. (a) State the coordinates of the vertex and the largest domain of the form $[k, \infty)$ for which $f(x)$ has an inverse function $f^{-1}(x)$. [2 marks]
  2. (b) Find an explicit equation for $f^{-1}(x)$ and state its domain and range. [2 marks]
View Worked Solution & Marking Rubric ▼

Part (a):

Complete the square: $f(x) = (x - 3)^2 - 9 + 5 = (x - 3)^2 - 4$. Vertex is at $(3, -4)$.

To pass the horizontal line test, the parabola must be restricted to one side of its axis of symmetry $x = 3$.

$$\therefore k = 3 \implies \text{Domain is } [3, \infty) \quad \text{[2 Marks]}$$

Part (b):

Let $y = (x - 3)^2 - 4$ for $x \ge 3, y \ge -4$. Swap $x$ and $y$:

$$x = (y - 3)^2 - 4 \implies (y - 3)^2 = x + 4 \implies y - 3 = +\sqrt{x + 4} \quad (\text{since } y \ge 3)$$ $$f^{-1}(x) = 3 + \sqrt{x + 4}$$

$$\text{Domain: } x \ge -4 \quad \text{Range: } y \ge 3 \quad \text{[2 Marks]}$$

Question 1.2 • Rational Inequalities & Invariance (Band 5/6) [4 Marks]

Solve the following rational inequalities algebraically:

  1. (a) $\frac{3x + 1}{x - 2} \le 2$ [2 marks]
  2. (b) $\frac{1}{x^2 - 4} \ge 1$ [2 marks]
View Worked Solution & Marking Rubric ▼

Part (a):

Note condition: $x \neq 2$. Multiply both sides by $(x - 2)^2 > 0$:

$$(3x + 1)(x - 2) \le 2(x - 2)^2$$ $$(x - 2)[(3x + 1) - 2(x - 2)] \le 0 \implies (x - 2)(x + 5) \le 0$$

Since $x \neq 2$, open bracket at 2:

$$-5 \le x < 2 \quad \text{[2 Marks]}$$

Part (b):

For $\frac{1}{x^2 - 4} \ge 1$, we require $x^2 - 4 > 0 \implies x < -2$ or $x > 2$.

Inverting both sides (as both are positive):

$$x^2 - 4 \le 1 \implies x^2 \le 5 \implies -\sqrt{5} \le x \le \sqrt{5}$$

Intersection with $x^2 > 4$:

$$\therefore -\sqrt{5} \le x < -2 \quad \text{or} \quad 2 < x \le \sqrt{5} \quad \text{[2 Marks]}$$

Question 1.3 • Modulus Curves & Roots Threshold (Band 6) [5 Marks]

Consider the modulus function $y = |x^2 - 4x| - 5$.

  1. (a) Find all $x$-intercepts and the $y$-intercept of the function. [2 marks]
  2. (b) Determine the exact coordinates of all stationary points / vertices. [2 marks]
  3. (c) Hence determine all values of constant $k$ for which $|x^2 - 4x| - 5 = k$ has exactly 4 distinct real solutions. [1 mark]
View Worked Solution & Marking Rubric ▼

Part (a):

• $y$-intercept: When $x=0$, $y = |0| - 5 = -5 \implies (0, -5)$.

• $x$-intercepts: $|x^2 - 4x| = 5$.

$$x^2 - 4x = 5 \implies x^2 - 4x - 5 = 0 \implies (x - 5)(x + 1) = 0 \implies x = 5, -1$$ $$x^2 - 4x = -5 \implies x^2 - 4x + 5 = 0 \implies \Delta = 16 - 20 = -4 < 0 \text{ (no real roots)}$$

$$\therefore x\text{-intercepts: } (-1, 0) \text{ and } (5, 0) \quad \text{[2 Marks]}$$

Part (b):

The vertex of $g(x) = x^2 - 4x$ is at $x = 2$, with value $g(2) = -4$.

Reflected by modulus: $|g(2)| = 4$. Shifting down by 5 gives local maximum at $(2, -1)$.

The cusps at $x = 0$ and $x = 4$ give local minima at $(0, -5)$ and $(4, -5)$.

$$\therefore \text{Minima at } (0, -5) \text{ and } (4, -5); \text{ Maximum at } (2, -1) \quad \text{[2 Marks]}$$

Part (c):

A horizontal line $y = k$ cuts 4 times strictly between the local minima and local maximum:

$$-5 < k < -1 \quad \text{[1 Mark]}$$

TOPIC 2

Trigonometric Functions & Calculus

Stage 6 Advanced Syllabus: MA-T1, MA-T2 (Year 11) & MA-T3 (Year 12)

HSC Weighting: ~14% – 18% (14 to 18 Marks)

Syllabus Sub-strands

  • • MA-T1: Radian measure, arc length $l = r\theta$, sector area $A = \frac{1}{2}r^2\theta$.
  • • MA-T2: Exact ratios, Pythagorean identities, sine/cosine rules.
  • • MA-T3: Graphs of $\sin, \cos, \tan$, amplitude/period, trig derivatives & integrals.

Core Competencies

  • • Solving non-linear trig equations across specified radian domains.
  • • Sinusoidal real-world modeling (tides, temperatures, daylight).
  • • Derivatives of $\sin(ax+b)$, $\cos(ax+b)$, $\sec^2(ax+b)$ and chain rule combinations.
  • • Reverse differentiation and finding exact area under trig curves.

Examination Role

Trigonometry frequently merges with optimization and differential modeling. Expect 2–3 multiple choice questions and a major 4–5 mark modeling calculus question in Section II.

Radians & Circular Geometry

• Conversion: $\pi\text{ rad} = 180^\circ \implies 1\text{ rad} = \frac{180}{\pi}^\circ$.
• Arc Length: $l = r\theta$ ($\theta$ in radians).
• Sector Area: $A = \frac{1}{2}r^2\theta$.
• Segment Area: $A_{\text{seg}} = \frac{1}{2}r^2(\theta - \sin\theta)$.
• Small angle approximations: as $\theta \to 0$, $\sin\theta \approx \theta$, $\tan\theta \approx \theta$.

Sinusoidal Graphs & Models

For $y = A\sin(B(x - C)) + D$ or $y = A\cos(B(x - C)) + D$:
• Amplitude: $|A| = \frac{\text{Max} - \text{Min}}{2}$.
• Vertical Shift / Mean: $D = \frac{\text{Max} + \text{Min}}{2}$.
• Period: $T = \frac{2\pi}{B}$ (for $\tan(Bx)$, period is $\frac{\pi}{B}$).
• Phase shift: $C$ units horizontally.

Trigonometric Calculus

  • • $\frac{d}{dx}\sin(f(x)) = f'(x)\cos(f(x))$
  • • $\frac{d}{dx}\cos(f(x)) = -f'(x)\sin(f(x))$
  • • $\frac{d}{dx}\tan(f(x)) = f'(x)\sec^2(f(x))$
  • • $\int \cos(ax+b)\,dx = \frac{1}{a}\sin(ax+b) + c$
  • • $\int \sin(ax+b)\,dx = -\frac{1}{a}\cos(ax+b) + c$
  • • $\int \sec^2(ax+b)\,dx = \frac{1}{a}\tan(ax+b) + c$

Provided on NESA Reference Sheet

  • $l = r\theta, \quad A = \frac{1}{2}r^2\theta$
  • $\frac{a}{\sin A} = \frac{b}{\sin B} = \frac{c}{\sin C}, \quad c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab\cos C$
  • $\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}ab\sin C$
  • $\frac{d}{dx}\sin(ax) = a\cos(ax), \quad \frac{d}{dx}\cos(ax) = -a\sin(ax)$
  • $\int \cos(ax)\,dx = \frac{1}{a}\sin(ax) + c, \quad \int \sin(ax)\,dx = -\frac{1}{a}\cos(ax) + c$

MUST MEMORISE (Not on Reference Sheet)

  • Pythagorean Identities: $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$, $1 + \tan^2\theta = \sec^2\theta$.
  • Tan Derivative: $\frac{d}{dx}\tan(ax) = a\sec^2(ax)$, $\int \sec^2(ax)\,dx = \frac{1}{a}\tan(ax) + c$.
  • Sinusoidal Period: Period $T = \frac{2\pi}{B}$ (for tan: $T = \frac{\pi}{B}$).
  • Exact Values: $\sin(\frac{\pi}{6}) = \frac{1}{2}$, $\cos(\frac{\pi}{4}) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$, $\tan(\frac{\pi}{3}) = \sqrt{3}$.

HSC Marker Insights & Common Pitfalls

  • Calculator in Degree Mode: A huge percentage of marks in calculus questions are forfeit because students leave their calculators in DEGREE mode instead of RADIAN mode. All calculus of trigonometric functions relies exclusively on radian measure.
  • Negative Sign on Cosine Differentiation: Forgetting that differentiating cosine produces a negative sign ($\frac{d}{dx}\cos x = -\sin x$) while integrating sine produces a negative ($\int \sin x\,dx = -\cos x$).
  • Boundary Conditions on Modeling: When asked for time intervals where tidal height exceeds a threshold, students frequently forget to check both cycles when the interval spans 24 hours.

Question 2.1 • Non-linear Radian Equations (Band 5) [4 Marks]

Find all exact solutions to the trigonometric equation: $$2\sin^2 x - 3\cos x = 0 \quad \text{for } 0 \le x \le 2\pi$$

View Worked Solution & Marking Rubric ▼

Step 1: Convert to single variable using Pythagorean identity

Substitute $\sin^2 x = 1 - \cos^2 x$:

$$2(1 - \cos^2 x) - 3\cos x = 0 \implies 2 - 2\cos^2 x - 3\cos x = 0$$ $$2\cos^2 x + 3\cos x - 2 = 0 \quad \text{[1 Mark]}$$

Step 2: Factorise the quadratic equation

$$(2\cos x - 1)(\cos x + 2) = 0$$ $$\cos x = \frac{1}{2} \quad \text{or} \quad \cos x = -2 \quad \text{[1 Mark]}$$

Step 3: Solve for $x$ in $[0, 2\pi]$

$\cos x = -2$ has no real solutions because $-1 \le \cos x \le 1$.

For $\cos x = \frac{1}{2}$, cosine is positive in Quadrants 1 and 4:

$$\text{Base angle: } \frac{\pi}{3} \implies x = \frac{\pi}{3} \quad \text{or} \quad x = 2\pi - \frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{5\pi}{3} \quad \text{[2 Marks]}$$

$$\therefore x = \frac{\pi}{3}, \, \frac{5\pi}{3}$$

Question 2.2 • Product Rule & Normal to Trigonometric Curves (Band 5/6) [4 Marks]

Consider the curve $y = x\sin(2x)$.

  1. (a) Show that $\frac{dy}{dx} = \sin(2x) + 2x\cos(2x)$. [1 mark]
  2. (b) Find the gradient of the tangent to the curve at the point where $x = \frac{\pi}{6}$. [2 marks]
  3. (c) Hence state the exact gradient of the normal to the curve at this point. [1 mark]
View Worked Solution & Marking Rubric ▼

Part (a):

Apply product rule with $u = x, v = \sin(2x) \implies u' = 1, v' = 2\cos(2x)$:

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = 1\cdot\sin(2x) + x\cdot(2\cos(2x)) = \sin(2x) + 2x\cos(2x) \quad \text{[1 Mark]}$$

Part (b):

Substitute $x = \frac{\pi}{6}$ into the derivative:

$$m_T = \sin\left(2 \cdot \frac{\pi}{6}\right) + 2\left(\frac{\pi}{6}\right)\cos\left(2 \cdot \frac{\pi}{6}\right) = \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{3}\right) + \frac{\pi}{3}\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{3}\right)$$ $$m_T = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} + \frac{\pi}{3}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} + \frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{3\sqrt{3} + \pi}{6} \quad \text{[2 Marks]}$$

Part (c):

The normal gradient is the negative reciprocal of tangent gradient:

$$m_N = -\frac{1}{m_T} = -\frac{6}{3\sqrt{3} + \pi} \quad \text{[1 Mark]}$$
Question 2.3 • Sinusoidal Tide Modeling & Safe Navigation (Band 6) [5 Marks]

The depth of water $D(t)$ in metres at the entrance of a coastal harbour is modeled by: $$D(t) = 4 + 2\cos\left(\frac{\pi t}{6}\right) \quad \text{for } 0 \le t \le 24$$ where $t$ represents elapsed hours after midnight ($t = 0$).

  1. (a) State the maximum water depth, minimum water depth, and the tidal period in hours. [1 mark]
  2. (b) A cargo vessel requires a minimum water depth of $5\text{ m}$ to enter safely. Find all time intervals during the first 12 hours ($0 \le t \le 12$) when the vessel can safely enter. [2 marks]
  3. (c) Determine the rate of change of water depth at $4:00\text{ am}$ ($t = 4$). Give exact value in $\text{m/h}$. [2 marks]
View Worked Solution & Marking Rubric ▼

Part (a):

$$\text{Max} = 4 + 2 = 6\text{ m}, \quad \text{Min} = 4 - 2 = 2\text{ m}$$ $$\text{Period } T = \frac{2\pi}{\pi/6} = 12\text{ hours} \quad \text{[1 Mark]}$$

Part (b):

Solve $D(t) \ge 5$ for $0 \le t \le 12$:

$$4 + 2\cos\left(\frac{\pi t}{6}\right) \ge 5 \implies \cos\left(\frac{\pi t}{6}\right) \ge \frac{1}{2}$$
$$\text{Base angle: } \frac{\pi}{3} \implies 0 \le \frac{\pi t}{6} \le \frac{\pi}{3} \quad \text{or} \quad \frac{5\pi}{3} \le \frac{\pi t}{6} \le 2\pi$$
$$0 \le t \le 2 \quad \text{or} \quad 10 \le t \le 12 \quad \text{[2 Marks]}$$

$$\therefore \text{Safe entry: 12:00 am to 2:00 am, and 10:00 am to 12:00 pm.}$$

$$D'(t) = 2\left(-\frac{\pi}{6}\sin\left(\frac{\pi t}{6}\right)\right) = -\frac{\pi}{3}\sin\left(\frac{\pi t}{6}\right)$$ $$D'(4) = -\frac{\pi}{3}\sin\left(\frac{2\pi}{3}\right) = -\frac{\pi}{3}\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\right) = -\frac{\pi\sqrt{3}}{6}\text{ m/h} \quad \text{[2 Marks]}$$

TOPIC 3

Differential Calculus & Optimization

Stage 6 Advanced Syllabus: MA-C1, MA-C2 (Year 11) & MA-C3 (Year 12)

HSC Weighting: ~18% – 22% (18 to 22 Marks)

Syllabus Sub-strands

  • • MA-C1: First principles $f'(x) = \lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}$.
  • • MA-C2: Product, quotient, chain rules, tangents & normals.
  • • MA-C3: Second derivative, points of inflection, global vs. local extrema, applied optimization problems.

Core Competencies

  • • Setting up geometric / physical objective functions in one variable.
  • • Rigorous testing of stationary points ($f''(x)$ or gradient neighborhood table).
  • • Verifying change in concavity across potential points of inflection.
  • • Interpreting rates of change from composite displacement / velocity contexts.

Examination Role

Differential calculus forms the central spine of the HSC Advanced exam. Applied optimization (containers, geometry, profits) routinely features as a 4-mark or 5-mark extended response in the second half of the paper.

Rules of Differentiation

  • • Product Rule: $(uv)' = u'v + uv'$
  • • Quotient Rule: $\left(\frac{u}{v}\right)' = \frac{u'v - uv'}{v^2}$
  • • Chain Rule: $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy}{du} \cdot \frac{du}{dx}$
  • • Composite: $\frac{d}{dx}[g(x)]^n = n[g(x)]^{n-1}g'(x)$
  • • Tangent: $y - y_1 = m_T(x - x_1)$ where $m_T = f'(x_1)$
  • • Normal: $m_N = -\frac{1}{m_T}$ (if $m_T \neq 0$)

Extrema & Concavity

• Stationary Point: $f'(x) = 0$.
• Nature by 2nd Derivative:
$\quad f''(x) > 0 \implies$ Concave UP $\implies$ Local Minimum.
$\quad f''(x) < 0 \implies$ Concave DOWN $\implies$ Local Maximum.
$\quad f''(x) = 0 \implies$ Inconclusive! Must use sign-change table.
• Point of Inflection: Requires $f''(x) = 0$ AND a genuine change in sign of $f''(x)$ across the point.

Optimization Protocol

  1. Draw a clear diagram & define all variables.
  2. Write down the formula for the quantity to optimize.
  3. Use constraint equations to eliminate extra variables.
  4. Differentiate and set $\frac{dQ}{dx} = 0$.
  5. Solve for the critical value.
  6. ALWAYS PROVE NATURE using $\frac{d^2Q}{dx^2}$ or a neighborhood gradient table.

Provided on NESA Reference Sheet

  • $\frac{d}{dx}(x^n) = nx^{n-1}$
  • $\frac{d}{dx}(uv) = u\frac{dv}{dx} + v\frac{du}{dx}$ (Product Rule)
  • $\frac{d}{dx}\left(\frac{u}{v}\right) = \frac{v\frac{du}{dx} - u\frac{dv}{dx}}{v^2}$ (Quotient Rule)
  • $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy}{du}\frac{du}{dx}$ (Chain Rule)

MUST MEMORISE (Not on Reference Sheet)

  • First Principles Formula: $f'(x) = \lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}$.
  • Point of Inflection Proof: Just showing $f''(x) = 0$ is NOT sufficient; you must test for a sign change.
  • Normal gradient formula: $m_N = -\frac{1}{f'(x)}$.
  • Second derivative test: Concave up ($f'' > 0$) $\implies$ minimum; Concave down ($f'' < 0$) $\implies$ maximum.

HSC Marker Insights & Common Pitfalls

  • Omitting Justification of Extrema: In optimization questions, finding the stationary point without proving it is a maximum or minimum via $f''(x)$ or a table of values loses the final mark.
  • Quotient Rule Order Error: In the quotient rule numerator, writing $u'v - v'u$ upside-down or reversing the subtraction order ($uv' - u'v$) destroys the entire derivative.
  • $f''(x) = 0$ is NOT automatically an inflection point: For $y = x^4$, $f''(0) = 0$, but the curve is concave UP on both sides, so $(0, 0)$ is a minimum, NOT an inflection point!

Question 3.1 • Quotient Rule & Tangent Equation (Band 5) [4 Marks]

Consider the curve defined by $y = \frac{2x - 3}{x^2 + 1}$.

  1. (a) Show that $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{-2x^2 + 6x + 2}{(x^2 + 1)^2}$. [2 marks]
  2. (b) Find the equation of the tangent to the curve at the point where it crosses the $y$-axis. Write your answer in general form $ax + by + c = 0$. [2 marks]
View Worked Solution & Marking Rubric ▼

Part (a):

Let $u = 2x - 3 \implies u' = 2$, and $v = x^2 + 1 \implies v' = 2x$:

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{u'v - uv'}{v^2} = \frac{2(x^2 + 1) - (2x - 3)(2x)}{(x^2 + 1)^2}$$ $$= \frac{2x^2 + 2 - (4x^2 - 6x)}{(x^2 + 1)^2} = \frac{-2x^2 + 6x + 2}{(x^2 + 1)^2} \quad \text{as required.} \quad \text{[2 Marks]}$$

Part (b):

Crosses $y$-axis when $x = 0 \implies y = \frac{-3}{1} = -3 \implies (0, -3)$.

Gradient at $x = 0$:

$$m_T = \frac{-2(0) + 6(0) + 2}{(0 + 1)^2} = \frac{2}{1} = 2 \quad \text{[1 Mark]}$$ $$y - (-3) = 2(x - 0) \implies y + 3 = 2x \implies 2x - y - 3 = 0 \quad \text{[1 Mark]}$$
Question 3.2 • Applied Optimization: Cylindrical Can (Band 6) [5 Marks]

A closed cylindrical container with radius $r\text{ cm}$ and height $h\text{ cm}$ is designed to hold a fixed volume of $500\pi\text{ cm}^3$. The metal used for the circular top and bottom costs $3\text{ cents/cm}^2$, while the metal for the curved side costs $2\text{ cents/cm}^2$.

  1. (a) Show that the total cost $C$ in cents is given by $C = 6\pi r^2 + \frac{2000\pi}{r}$. [2 marks]
  2. (b) Find the exact value of $r$ that minimises the total cost of materials, and prove that it gives an absolute minimum cost. [3 marks]
View Worked Solution & Marking Rubric ▼

Part (a):

Volume: $V = \pi r^2 h = 500\pi \implies h = \frac{500}{r^2}$.

$$\text{Cost } C = 3 \times (2\pi r^2) + 2 \times (2\pi r h) = 6\pi r^2 + 4\pi r\left(\frac{500}{r^2}\right)$$ $$C = 6\pi r^2 + \frac{2000\pi}{r} \quad \text{as required.} \quad \text{[2 Marks]}$$

Part (b):

Differentiate with respect to $r$:

$$\frac{dC}{dr} = 12\pi r - \frac{2000\pi}{r^2}$$ $$\text{Set } \frac{dC}{dr} = 0 \implies 12\pi r = \frac{2000\pi}{r^2} \implies r^3 = \frac{2000}{12} = \frac{500}{3}$$ $$r = \sqrt[3]{\frac{500}{3}} \text{ cm} \quad \text{[2 Marks]}$$

Prove Minimum via Second Derivative:

Question 3.3 • Transcendental Inflection Points & Concavity (Band 6) [5 Marks]

Integral Calculus & Area

Stage 6 Advanced Syllabus: MA-C4 (Year 12)

HSC Weighting: ~16% – 20% (16 to 20 Marks)

Syllabus Sub-strands

  • • MA-C4.1: Indefinite anti-derivatives and initial value boundary problems.
  • • MA-C4.2: Definite integrals, Fundamental Theorem $\int_a^b f(x)dx = [F(x)]_a^b$.
  • • MA-C4.2: Physical area enclosed between curves & axes, Composite Trapezoidal Rule.

Core Competencies

  • • Identifying functions above vs. below the $x$-axis to avoid signed cancellation.
  • • Finding curve intersection points algebraically prior to area evaluation.
  • • Reverse chain rule integration: $\int f'(x)[f(x)]^n dx$ and $\int \frac{f'(x)}{f(x)}dx$.
  • • Numerical estimation via the composite Trapezoidal Rule formula.

Examination Role

Integration is tested both directly and in synthesis with probability (continuous PDFs). Exact area between intersecting curves is one of the most reliable multi-mark questions in the HSC exam.

Integration Foundations

  • • $\int x^n\,dx = \frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1} + c \quad (n \neq -1)$
  • • $\int (ax+b)^n\,dx = \frac{(ax+b)^{n+1}}{a(n+1)} + c$
  • • Reverse Chain: $\int f'(x)[f(x)]^n\,dx = \frac{[f(x)]^{n+1}}{n+1} + c$
  • • Log Form: $\int \frac{f'(x)}{f(x)}\,dx = \ln|f(x)| + c$

Geometric Area Calculations

• Area Above $x$-axis: $\int_a^b f(x)\,dx \ge 0$.
• Area Below $x$-axis: $\int_a^b f(x)\,dx < 0 \implies \text{Area} = -\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$.
• Split Integrals: Always split at roots: $\int_{x_1}^{x_2} - \int_{x_2}^{x_3}$.
• Area Between Curves:
$$\text{Area} = \int_a^b [\text{Top}(x) - \text{Bottom}(x)]\,dx$$

Composite Trapezoidal Rule

For $n$ subintervals of equal width $h = \frac{b - a}{n}$ with $n + 1$ function values: $$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx \approx \frac{h}{2} \left[ y_0 + y_n + 2(y_1 + y_2 + \dots + y_{n-1}) \right]$$ • Tip: "5 function values" means $n = 4$ subintervals!

Provided on NESA Reference Sheet

  • $\int x^n\,dx = \frac{1}{n+1}x^{n+1} + c$
  • $\int (ax+b)^n\,dx = \frac{1}{a(n+1)}(ax+b)^{n+1} + c$
  • $\int \frac{1}{x}\,dx = \ln|x| + c, \quad \int \frac{f'(x)}{f(x)}\,dx = \ln|f(x)| + c$
  • $\int_a^b f(x)\,dx \approx \frac{b-a}{2n}\left\{ f(a) + f(b) + 2\sum_{i=1}^{n-1} f(x_i) \right\}$

MUST MEMORISE (Not on Reference Sheet)

  • Area between two curves: $\int_a^b [f(x) - g(x)]\,dx$ where $f(x) \ge g(x)$.
  • Splitting at $x$-axis crossings: Integrals evaluate to net signed area, not physical area!
  • Constant of integration $+c$: Mandatory for every indefinite anti-derivative.
  • Odd integrand symmetry: $\int_{-a}^a f(x)\,dx = 0$ if $f(-x) = -f(x)$.

HSC Marker Insights & Common Pitfalls

  • Net Signed Area vs. Geometric Area: Evaluating $\int_0^{2\pi} \sin x\,dx = 0$ when asked for the area enclosed between $y = \sin x$ and the $x$-axis from $0$ to $2\pi$ (correct answer is $2 + 2 = 4\text{ sq. units}$).
  • Strip Width $h$ Confusion: Miscounting $n$ in the trapezoidal rule. A question giving 3 function values has 2 subintervals ($n=2$), NOT 3!
  • Missing Absolute Value in Log Integrals: Writing $\ln(x)$ instead of $\ln|x|$, which loses marks when evaluating over intervals where the argument is negative.

Stage 6 Advanced Syllabus: MA-E1 (Year 11), MA-C2, MA-C4 & MA-M2 (Year 12)

HSC Weighting: ~16% – 20% (16 to 20 Marks)

Syllabus Sub-strands

  • • MA-E1: Index & Log laws, change of base, exponential/log equations.
  • • MA-C2.2/C4: Calculus of $e^{f(x)}$ and $\ln(f(x))$, integrals yielding $\ln|x|$.
  • • MA-M2: Exponential Growth & Decay, Newton's Law of Cooling differential models.

Core Competencies

  • • Manipulating log expressions prior to differentiation to avoid complex chain rules.
  • • Solving $\frac{dN}{dt} = kN \implies N(t) = N_0 e^{kt}$ and evaluating half-lives.
  • • Setting up modified decay equations $\frac{dT}{dt} = -k(T - T_{\text{env}})$.
  • • Curve sketching with horizontal ($y = 0$) and vertical ($x = 0$) asymptotes.

Examination Role

Exponentials and logarithmic modeling feature heavily in every HSC paper. Almost always presents a high-mark differential equation scenario (cooling, radioactive decay, population saturation).

Log Laws & Inversion

  • • $\log_a(xy) = \log_a x + \log_a y$
  • • $\log_a\left(\frac{x}{y}\right) = \log_a x - \log_a y$
  • • $\log_a(x^p) = p\log_a x$
  • • $\log_b a = \frac{\ln a}{\ln b}$ (Change of Base)
  • • Inverse Pair: $y = e^x \iff x = \ln y$
  • • Domain: $\ln(f(x))$ strictly requires $f(x) > 0$!

Exp & Log Calculus

  • • $\frac{d}{dx}[e^{f(x)}] = f'(x)e^{f(x)}$
  • • $\frac{d}{dx}[\ln(f(x))] = \frac{f'(x)}{f(x)}$
  • • $\int e^{ax+b}\,dx = \frac{1}{a}e^{ax+b} + c$
  • • $\int \frac{1}{ax+b}\,dx = \frac{1}{a}\ln|ax+b| + c$
  • • Pro-Tip: Expand $\ln\left(\frac{x^3}{\sqrt{x+1}}\right)$ to $3\ln x - \frac{1}{2}\ln(x+1)$ before differentiating!

Growth & Cooling Models

• Natural Growth/Decay:
$$\frac{dN}{dt} = kN \iff N(t) = N_0 e^{kt}$$ • Newton's Law of Cooling:
$$\frac{dT}{dt} = -k(T - T_{\text{env}}) \iff T(t) = T_{\text{env}} + Ae^{-kt}$$ • $T(0) = T_{\text{env}} + A \implies A = T(0) - T_{\text{env}}$.
• Limiting asymptote: as $t \to \infty$, $T(t) \to T_{\text{env}}$.

Provided on NESA Reference Sheet

  • $\frac{d}{dx}(e^{kx}) = ke^{kx}, \quad \frac{d}{dx}(\ln x) = \frac{1}{x}$
  • $\int e^{kx}\,dx = \frac{1}{k}e^{kx} + c$
  • $\int \frac{1}{x}\,dx = \ln|x| + c, \quad \int \frac{f'(x)}{f(x)}\,dx = \ln|f(x)| + c$

MUST MEMORISE (Not on Reference Sheet)

  • All Log Laws: Product, quotient, power, and change of base $\log_b a = \frac{\ln a}{\ln b}$.
  • Chain Rule Generalisations: $\frac{d}{dx}[e^{f(x)}] = f'(x)e^{f(x)}$, $\frac{d}{dx}[\ln(f(x))] = \frac{f'(x)}{f(x)}$.
  • Differential Equation Solution: $\frac{dN}{dt} = kN \iff N = N_0 e^{kt}$.
  • Half-Life Formula: $t_{1/2} = \frac{\ln 2}{k}$ (for decay parameter $k > 0$).

HSC Marker Insights & Common Pitfalls

  • Negative Log Arguments: Forgetting that $\ln(u)$ is undefined for $u \le 0$. When solving log equations that reduce to quadratics, students must discard negative or out-of-domain solutions.
  • Missing $+ T_{\text{env}}$ in Cooling Verification: When asked to verify that $T = 20 + Ae^{-kt}$ satisfies $\frac{dT}{dt} = -k(T - 20)$, students often differentiate incorrectly or substitute $T$ without subtracting the room temperature.
  • Derivative of $\ln(kx)$: Believing $\frac{d}{dx}\ln(5x) = \frac{5}{5x} \cdot 5 = \frac{5}{x}$. The $5$ cancels: $\frac{d}{dx}\ln(5x) = \frac{5}{5x} = \frac{1}{x}$.

Financial Mathematics & Series

Stage 6 Advanced Syllabus: MA-M1 (Year 12)

HSC Weighting: ~12% – 15% (12 to 15 Marks)

Syllabus Sub-strands

  • • MA-M1.1: Arithmetic Progressions (AP): $T_n = a + (n-1)d$, $S_n$.
  • • MA-M1.2: Geometric Progressions (GP): $T_n = ar^{n-1}$, $S_n$, limiting sum $S_\infty = \frac{a}{1-r}$.
  • • MA-M1.3: Financial applications: Compound interest, investments, annuities, reducing-balance loans.

Core Competencies

  • • Establishing rigorous multi-line financial recurrence relations ($A_1, A_2, \dots, A_n$).
  • • Summing geometric series for regular deposits and loan amortization tables.
  • • Converting annual percentage rates to monthly/quarterly compounding periods.
  • • Solving for installment $M$, balance $A_n$, or number of repayment periods $n$ using logs.

Examination Role

Financial mathematics almost always delivers a guaranteed 4 to 6 mark structured question in Section II (typically Q13, Q14, or Q15), demanding full proof steps without skipping lines.

AP & GP Foundations

  • • AP: $T_n = a + (n - 1)d$
  • • AP: $S_n = \frac{n}{2}[2a + (n - 1)d]$
  • • GP: $T_n = ar^{n-1}$
  • • GP: $S_n = \frac{a(r^n - 1)}{r - 1} = \frac{a(1 - r^n)}{1 - r}$
  • • Limiting Sum: $S_\infty = \frac{a}{1 - r}$ exists $\iff |r| < 1$

Annuities & Future Value

Deposit $\$M$ at start of each compounding period with growth factor $R = 1 + r$:
• 1st installment grows for $n$ periods: $M R^n$.
• Last installment grows for $1$ period: $M R^1$.
• Future Value is a GP with first term $a = MR$, ratio $r = R$:
$$A_n = MR + MR^2 + \dots + MR^n = \frac{MR(R^n - 1)}{R - 1}$$

Loan Repayments Protocol

Borrow $\$P$ at rate $r$ per period, repaying $\$M$ at end of each period:
• $A_1 = P R - M$
• $A_2 = A_1 R - M = P R^2 - M(R + 1)$
• $A_n = P R^n - M(R^{n-1} + R^{n-2} + \dots + 1)$
• Summing GP inside bracket:
$$A_n = P R^n - M \frac{R^n - 1}{R - 1}$$ • Fully repaid when $A_n = 0$.

Provided on NESA Reference Sheet

  • $T_n = a + (n-1)d, \quad S_n = \frac{n}{2}[2a + (n-1)d] = \frac{n}{2}(a + l)$
  • $T_n = ar^{n-1}, \quad S_n = \frac{a(r^n - 1)}{r - 1} = \frac{a(1 - r^n)}{1 - r}$
  • $S_\infty = \frac{a}{1 - r} \quad (|r| < 1)$

MUST MEMORISE (Not on Reference Sheet)

  • Recurrence line generation: Must be able to derive $A_1, A_2, \dots, A_n$ step-by-step.
  • Compounding Period Conversions: Monthly: $r = \frac{i}{12}$, $n = 12 \times \text{years}$.
  • Loan payoff condition: $A_n = 0 \implies PR^n = M\frac{R^n - 1}{R - 1}$.
  • Limiting sum convergence test: Stating $|r| < 1$ before computing $S_\infty$.

HSC Marker Insights & Common Pitfalls

  • Skipping the Recurrence Steps: In "Show that the amount owing after $n$ months is..." questions, jumping straight to the final GP formula without showing $A_1, A_2$ and the series pattern receives 0 or 1 mark out of 3. Markers demand the algebraic induction steps!
  • Timing of Payments: Confusing whether money is invested at the *beginning* of each period (annuity in advance, $a = MR$) vs. at the *end* of each period (annuity in arrears, $a = M$).
  • Rounding Prematurely: Rounding intermediate interest rates (e.g. $1.004166...$) causes substantial dollar compounding discrepancies in the final repayment figure. Keep values in calculator memory.

  2. (b) Calculate the monthly repayment $\$M$ required to completely pay off the loan in 30 years. [2 marks]
View Worked Solution & Marking Rubric ▼

Part (a):

Monthly rate $r = \frac{0.048}{12} = 0.004 \implies R = 1.004$. Principal $P = 600\,000$.

$$A_1 = P(1.004) - M \quad \text{[1 Mark]}$$
$$A_2 = A_1(1.004) - M = [P(1.004) - M](1.004) - M = P(1.004)^2 - M(1.004 + 1)$$
$$A_n = P(1.004)^n - M(1 + 1.004 + 1.004^2 + \dots + 1.004^{n-1}) \quad \text{[1 Mark]}$$
$$\text{Summing GP inside: } S = \frac{1(1.004^n - 1)}{1.004 - 1} = \frac{1.004^n - 1}{0.004} = 250(1.004^n - 1)$$
$$A_n = 600\,000(1.004)^n - 250M(1.004^n - 1) \quad \text{as required.} \quad \text{[1 Mark]}$$

Part (b):

Loan repaid when $A_{360} = 0$:

$$600\,000(1.004)^{360} - 250M(1.004^{360} - 1) = 0$$
$$M = \frac{600\,000(1.004)^{360}}{250(1.004^{360} - 1)} = \frac{2400(1.004)^{360}}{1.004^{360} - 1} \quad \text{[1 Mark]}$$
$$M \approx \frac{2400(4.20864)}{3.20864} \approx \$3148.06\text{ per month} \quad \text{[1 Mark]}$$
Question 6.3 • Arithmetic Series & Bouncing Ball Limiting Sum (Band 6) [5 Marks]

This question assesses both Arithmetic Progressions and Infinite Geometric Series applications.

  1. (a) A runner trains for a marathon by running $5\text{ km}$ on Day 1, and increasing daily distance by $0.8\text{ km}$ each subsequent day. On which day will the runner first exceed $25\text{ km}$? What is the total distance completed over the first 30 days? [2 marks]
  2. (b) An elastic ball is dropped from a height of $12\text{ m}$. After each bounce, it rebounds to $\frac{2}{3}$ of its preceding height. Prove that the total vertical distance traveled before coming to rest is $60\text{ m}$. [3 marks]
View Worked Solution & Marking Rubric ▼

Part (a):

$$T_n = 5 + (n - 1)(0.8) > 25 \implies 0.8(n - 1) > 20 \implies n - 1 > 25 \implies n > 26 \implies \text{Day 27} \quad \text{[1 Mark]}$$ $$S_{30} = \frac{30}{2}[2(5) + 29(0.8)] = 15[10 + 23.2] = 15(33.2) = 498\text{ km} \quad \text{[1 Mark]}$$

Part (b):

Initial drop: $12\text{ m}$. Every bounce travels up and down:

$$D = 12 + 2\left[ 12\left(\frac{2}{3}\right) + 12\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^2 + \dots \right] = 12 + 2\left[ 8 + 8\left(\frac{2}{3}\right) + \dots \right] \quad \text{[1 Mark]}$$
$$\text{Inside is infinite GP with } a = 8, r = \frac{2}{3} < 1: \quad S_\infty = \frac{8}{1 - 2/3} = \frac{8}{1/3} = 24\text{ m} \quad \text{[1 Mark]}$$
$$D = 12 + 2(24) = 12 + 48 = 60\text{ m} \quad \text{as required.} \quad \text{[1 Mark]}$$

TOPIC 7

Statistical Analysis & Probability

Stage 6 Advanced Syllabus: MA-S1 (Year 11), MA-S2 & MA-S3 (Year 12)

HSC Weighting: ~16% – 20% (16 to 20 Marks)

Syllabus Sub-strands

  • • MA-S1: Probability axioms, conditional probability $P(A|B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}$, Venn diagrams.
  • • MA-S2: Discrete Random Variables, expected value $E(X)$, variance $\text{Var}(X) = E(X^2) - \mu^2$.
  • • MA-S3.1/S3.2: Continuous Random Variables, PDF/CDF, Normal Distribution & $z$-scores.

Core Competencies

  • • Proving a function is a valid PDF: $f(x) \ge 0$ AND $\int_{-\infty}^\infty f(x)dx = 1$.
  • • Calculating continuous expected value $\int x f(x)dx$ and median $\int_{-\infty}^m f(x)dx = 0.5$.
  • • Connecting CDF and PDF via calculus: $f(x) = F'(x) = \frac{d}{dx}F(x)$.
  • • Applying the 68–95–99.7% empirical rule and interpreting standardized $z$-scores.

Examination Role

Statistics and probability questions feature across both Multiple Choice and Section II. Continuous distributions synthesized with calculus integrals routinely serve as Band 5–6 discriminators.

Discrete Random Variables

  • • $\sum P(X = x) = 1$ and $0 \le P(X = x) \le 1$
  • • Mean / Expected Value: $\mu = E(X) = \sum x P(X = x)$
  • • $E(X^2) = \sum x^2 P(X = x)$
  • • $\text{Var}(X) = E(X^2) - [E(X)]^2$
  • • Standard Deviation: $\sigma = \sqrt{\text{Var}(X)}$
  • • Fair game: Net expected profit $= \$0$

Continuous RVs & CDF

• PDF Axioms: $f(x) \ge 0$ and $\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = 1$.
• Point Probability: $P(X = c) = 0$ for any exact value!
• Probability Interval: $P(c \le X \le d) = \int_c^d f(x)\,dx$.
• Mean: $\mu = \int_a^b x f(x)\,dx$.
• Variance: $\text{Var}(X) = \int_a^b x^2 f(x)\,dx - \mu^2$.
• CDF: $F(x) = \int_a^x f(t)\,dt \implies f(x) = F'(x)$.

Normal Distribution & Z-Scores

• Standardised $z$-score: $z = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma}$.
• Empirical Rule (68-95-99.7):
$\quad \mu \pm 1\sigma \implies 68\%$ ($34\%$ each side).
$\quad \mu \pm 2\sigma \implies 95\%$ ($47.5\%$ each side).
$\quad \mu \pm 3\sigma \implies 99.7\%$.
• Upper tail $> \mu + 2\sigma$ is $2.5\%$.
• Bivariate regression line: $y = mx + c$ with correlation $r$.

Provided on NESA Reference Sheet

  • $P(a \le X \le b) = \int_a^b f(x)\,dx$

MUST MEMORISE (Not on Reference Sheet)

  • CDF Calculus Link: $f(x) = \frac{d}{dx}F(x) = F'(x)$ and $F(x) = \int_{-\infty}^x f(t)\,dt$.
  • Continuous Point Probability: $P(X = c) = 0 \implies P(a \le X \le b) = P(a < X < b)$.
  • Empirical 68-95-99.7 Rule: Exact percentages for $\pm 1\sigma, \pm 2\sigma, \pm 3\sigma$.
  • Conditional Probability Formula: $P(A|B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}$.

HSC Marker Insights & Common Pitfalls

  • PDF Validation Requires TWO Conditions: If asked to "show that $f(x)$ is a probability density function", students frequently only evaluate $\int f(x)dx = 1$ and fail to state that $f(x) \ge 0$, forfeiting an easy mark.
  • Variance Square Root: In both discrete and continuous calculations, students frequently calculate $\text{Var}(X)$ and forget to take the square root $\sigma = \sqrt{\text{Var}(X)}$ when asked for standard deviation.
  • Extrapolation Warnings: In bivariate data, students often substitute an $x$-value far beyond the measured data domain without explaining that the estimate is unreliable due to *extrapolation*.

Question 7.1 • Continuous Probability Density & Median (Band 5) [5 Marks]

A continuous random variable $X$ has probability density function: $$f(x) = \begin{cases} k(4x - x^2), & 0 \le x \le 4 \\ 0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

  1. (a) Show that $k = \frac{3}{32}$. [2 marks]
  2. (b) Calculate the expected value $E(X)$. [1 mark]
  3. (c) Calculate the conditional probability $P(X \ge 3 \mid X \ge 2)$. [2 marks]
View Worked Solution & Marking Rubric ▼

Part (a):

For a valid PDF, $\int_0^4 k(4x - x^2)\,dx = 1$ and $f(x) \ge 0$ on $[0, 4]$:

$$k \left[ 2x^2 - \frac{x^3}{3} \right]_0^4 = 1 \implies k \left( 32 - \frac{64}{3} \right) = 1 \implies k\left(\frac{32}{3}\right) = 1 \implies k = \frac{3}{32} \quad \text{[2 Marks]}$$

Part (b):

By symmetry of the downward parabola on $[0, 4]$, or by integration:

$$E(X) = \frac{3}{32}\int_0^4 (4x^2 - x^3)\,dx = \frac{3}{32}\left[ \frac{4x^3}{3} - \frac{x^4}{4} \right]_0^4 = \frac{3}{32}\left( \frac{256}{3} - 64 \right) = 2 \quad \text{[1 Mark]}$$

Part (c):

Recall $P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)} \implies P(X \ge 3 \mid X \ge 2) = \frac{P(X \ge 3)}{P(X \ge 2)}$.

By symmetry, $P(X \ge 2) = 0.5$.

$$P(X \ge 3) = \frac{3}{32}\int_3^4 (4x - x^2)\,dx = \frac{3}{32}\left[ 2x^2 - \frac{x^3}{3} \right]_3^4 = \frac{3}{32}\left( \frac{5}{3} \right) = \frac{5}{32}$$ $$P(X \ge 3 \mid X \ge 2) = \frac{5/32}{1/2} = \frac{5}{16} = 0.3125 \quad \text{[2 Marks]}$$
Question 7.2 • Cumulative Distribution Function & Probability (Band 6) [4 Marks]

The cumulative distribution function (CDF) of continuous random variable $Y$ is given by: $$F(y) = \begin{cases} 0, & y < 0 \\ \frac{y^3}{27}, & 0 \le y \le 3 \\ 1, & y > 3 \end{cases}$$

  1. (a) Find the probability density function $f(y)$. [1 mark]
  2. (b) Determine the median value $m$ of the distribution. [2 marks]
  3. (c) Find $P(1 \le Y \le 2)$. [1 mark]
View Worked Solution & Marking Rubric ▼

Part (a):

$$f(y) = \frac{d}{dy}F(y) = \frac{d}{dy}\left(\frac{y^3}{27}\right) = \frac{3y^2}{27} = \frac{y^2}{9} \quad \text{for } 0 \le y \le 3 \quad \text{[1 Mark]}$$

Part (b):

Median satisfies $F(m) = 0.5$:

$$\frac{m^3}{27} = \frac{1}{2} \implies m^3 = \frac{27}{2} = 13.5 \implies m = \sqrt[3]{13.5} = \frac{3}{\sqrt[3]{2}} \approx 2.381 \quad \text{[2 Marks]}$$

Part (c):

$$P(1 \le Y \le 2) = F(2) - F(1) = \frac{2^3}{27} - \frac{1^3}{27} = \frac{8 - 1}{27} = \frac{7}{27} \quad \text{[1 Mark]}$$
Question 7.3 • Normal Standardisation & Bivariate Regression (Band 6) [5 Marks]

This question assesses both continuous normal distributions and bivariate regression modeling.

  1. (a) In the HSC Trial exams, Mathematics marks are normally distributed with $\mu_M = 64$ and $\sigma_M = 10$. Physics marks are normally distributed with $\mu_P = 58$ and $\sigma_P = 8$. Sophia scored 82 in Mathematics and 74 in Physics. Calculate both $z$-scores and identify in which subject she achieved a better relative performance. [2 marks]
  2. (b) If the top $2.5\%$ of students in Mathematics receive a Certificate of Academic Excellence, use the empirical rule to determine the minimum qualifying score. [1 mark]
  3. (c) A researcher models the relationship between weekly homework hours ($x$) and trial exam score ($y$) using the least-squares regression line $y = 2.5x + 45$.
    • (i) Interpret the gradient $m = 2.5$ in the context of the study. [1 mark]
    • (ii) Explain whether this model can reliably predict a student's score if they study $35\text{ hours/week}$. [1 mark]
View Worked Solution & Marking Rubric ▼

Part (a):

$$z_M = \frac{82 - 64}{10} = \frac{18}{10} = +1.80 \quad \text{[1 Mark]}$$
$$z_P = \frac{74 - 58}{8} = \frac{16}{8} = +2.00 \quad \text{[1 Mark]}$$

$$\therefore \text{Sophia performed relatively better in Physics ($z = +2.00 > +1.80$).}$$

Part (b):

The top $2.5\%$ cutoff corresponds to $z = +2$ ($\mu + 2\sigma$):

$$\text{Cutoff} = 64 + 2(10) = 84 \quad \text{[1 Mark]}$$

Part (c):

(i) Gradient Interpretation: For each additional 1 hour spent studying per week, a student's trial exam score is predicted to increase on average by $2.5$ marks. [1 Mark]

(ii) Reliability (Extrapolation): Predicting for 35 hours/week would yield $y = 2.5(35) + 45 = 132.5$, which exceeds $100\%$. 35 hours per week is far outside the reasonable domain of collected data (an act of extreme extrapolation), making the prediction unreliable and invalid. [1 Mark]
