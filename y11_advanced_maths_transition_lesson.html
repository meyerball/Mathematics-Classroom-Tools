Topic 1

Calculus: The Quotient Rule & Tangent Equation

3 Marks

Consider the function \( f(x) = \frac{2x - 1}{x^2 + 1} \).

(a) Show that the derivative is given by \( f'(x) = \frac{-2x^2 + 2x + 2}{(x^2 + 1)^2} \).
(b) Hence, find the exact equation of the tangent to the curve at the point where \( x = 1 \).

Hint: Let \( u = 2x - 1 \) and \( v = x^2 + 1 \). Apply \( \frac{v u' - u v'}{v^2} \). Watch the negative sign when distributing \( -u v' \)!
Worked Solution

Part (a): Let \( u = 2x - 1 \implies u' = 2 \), and \( v = x^2 + 1 \implies v' = 2x \).

Applying the Quotient Rule:

\[ f'(x) = \frac{(x^2 + 1)(2) - (2x - 1)(2x)}{(x^2 + 1)^2} = \frac{2x^2 + 2 - (4x^2 - 2x)}{(x^2 + 1)^2} = \frac{-2x^2 + 2x + 2}{(x^2 + 1)^2} \]

Part (b): At \( x = 1 \):

Evaluate \( y \)-coordinate: \( y = f(1) = \frac{2(1) - 1}{1^2 + 1} = \frac{1}{2} \). So the point is \( \left(1, \frac{1}{2}\right) \).

Evaluate gradient \( m \): \( m = f'(1) = \frac{-2(1)^2 + 2(1) + 2}{(1^2 + 1)^2} = \frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{2} \).

Using point-gradient form \( y - y_1 = m(x - x_1) \):

\[ y - \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{2}(x - 1) \implies y = \frac{1}{2}x \quad \text{or} \quad x - 2y = 0 \]
Topic 2

Trigonometry: Solving Multi-Quadrant Equations

3 Marks

Solve the equation \( 2\cos^2\theta - \cos\theta - 1 = 0 \) for the domain \( 0 \le \theta \le 2\pi \).

Give your answers as exact values in terms of \( \pi \).

Hint: Treat this as a quadratic equation in \( u = \cos\theta \): \( 2u^2 - u - 1 = 0 \). Factorize, then use the ASTC quadrant rule for the unit circle.
Worked Solution

Factor the quadratic: \( (2\cos\theta + 1)(\cos\theta - 1) = 0 \).

This gives two branches:

  • Branch 1: \( \cos\theta = 1 \implies \theta = 0, 2\pi \) (at boundary endpoints).
  • Branch 2: \( \cos\theta = -\frac{1}{2} \). The reference angle is \( \frac{\pi}{3} \). Cosine is negative in Quadrants II and III: \[ \theta = \pi - \frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{2\pi}{3}, \quad \text{and} \quad \theta = \pi + \frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{4\pi}{3} \]
\[ \therefore \theta = 0, \; \frac{2\pi}{3}, \; \frac{4\pi}{3}, \; 2\pi \]
Topic 3

Functions: Even/Odd Symmetry & Maximal Domain

3 Marks

Let \( g(x) = \frac{x}{\sqrt{16 - x^2}} \).

(a) State the maximal real domain of \( g(x) \).
(b) Prove algebraically whether \( g(x) \) is an even function, an odd function, or neither.

Hint: For domain, the denominator cannot be zero AND the expression under the square root must be strictly positive: \( 16 - x^2 > 0 \). For symmetry, compute \( g(-x) \) and compare with \( -g(x) \).
Worked Solution

Part (a): Domain

Because \( \sqrt{16 - x^2} \) is in the denominator, we require: \( 16 - x^2 > 0 \implies x^2 < 16 \implies -4 < x < 4 \).

Hence, the domain is \( x \in (-4, 4) \) or \( \{ x \in \mathbb{R} : -4 < x < 4 \} \).

Part (b): Symmetry Proof

Evaluate \( g(-x) \):

\[ g(-x) = \frac{-x}{\sqrt{16 - (-x)^2}} = \frac{-x}{\sqrt{16 - x^2}} = -\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{16 - x^2}}\right) = -g(x) \]

Since \( g(-x) = -g(x) \) for all \( x \) in the domain, \( g(x) \) is an odd function (possessing point rotational symmetry of \( 180^\circ \) about the origin).

Topic 4

Calculus: Composite Functions & The Chain Rule

3 Marks

Differentiate \( y = (3x^2 - 5x)^4 \) with respect to \( x \), and hence find all points on the curve where the tangent is horizontal.

Hint: Chain rule: \( \frac{dy}{dx} = n[u(x)]^{n-1} \cdot u'(x) \). Horizontal tangent means \( \frac{dy}{dx} = 0 \). Factorise completely!
Worked Solution

Let \( u = 3x^2 - 5x \implies \frac{du}{dx} = 6x - 5 \).

Then \( y = u^4 \implies \frac{dy}{du} = 4u^3 \).

\[ \frac{dy}{dx} = 4(3x^2 - 5x)^3 (6x - 5) \]

For a horizontal tangent, set \( \frac{dy}{dx} = 0 \):

Notice that \( 3x^2 - 5x = x(3x - 5) \). So: \[ 4 [x(3x - 5)]^3 (6x - 5) = 4x^3(3x - 5)^3(6x - 5) = 0 \]

This yields roots at: \[ x = 0, \quad x = \frac{5}{3}, \quad x = \frac{5}{6} \]

Corresponding \( y \)-values:

  • When \( x = 0 \implies y = 0 \implies (0, 0) \)
  • When \( x = \frac{5}{3} \implies 3(5/3)^2 - 5(5/3) = 0 \implies y = 0 \implies \left(\frac{5}{3}, 0\right) \)
  • When \( x = \frac{5}{6} \implies y = \left(3\cdot\frac{25}{36} - \frac{25}{6}\right)^4 = \left(-\frac{25}{12}\right)^4 = \frac{390625}{20736} \approx 18.84 \)
Topic 5

Quadratics: The Discriminant & Tangency Conditions

3 Marks

Find the values of \( k \) for which the line \( y = 2x + k \) is tangent to the parabola \( y = x^2 - 4x + 7 \).

Hint: Equate both equations to form a quadratic in \( x \). For tangency (exactly one intersection point), set the discriminant \( \Delta = b^2 - 4ac = 0 \).
Worked Solution

Equating the curves:

\[ x^2 - 4x + 7 = 2x + k \implies x^2 - 6x + (7 - k) = 0 \]

For the line to be tangent to the parabola, there must be exactly one intersection, meaning \( \Delta = 0 \):

Here \( a = 1, b = -6, c = 7 - k \):

\[ \Delta = (-6)^2 - 4(1)(7 - k) = 36 - 28 + 4k = 8 + 4k \]

Setting \( \Delta = 0 \):

\[ 8 + 4k = 0 \implies 4k = -8 \implies k = -2 \]

Alternative calculus method: Set gradient \( \frac{dy}{dx} = 2x - 4 = 2 \implies x = 3 \). Then \( y = 3^2 - 4(3) + 7 = 4 \). Plug into line: \( 4 = 2(3) + k \implies k = -2 \). (Great way to cross-verify!)

Sneak Peek

The Gateway to Year 12

Here is exactly why today's lesson matters: Year 12 does not replace Year 11—it multiplies it.

Calculus → Integral Calculus

In Y12, you will reverse differentiation to find areas under curves. If you cannot spot the chain rule or reverse power rule rapidly, integration becomes double the work.

Trigonometry → Trig Derivatives

In Term 1 of Y12, you will find \( \frac{d}{dx}[\sin(x)] = \cos(x) \). Solving stationary points requires solving trig equations across \( [0, 2\pi] \) instantly!

Functions → Curve Sketching

Second derivatives \( f''(x) \), points of inflection, concavity, and asymptotes combine every single graph rule learned in Year 11.

Pocket Reference: Year 11 Must-Know Rules

Keep for Year 12 Term 1

1. Product Rule

\( (uv)' = u'v + uv' \)

Differentiate the first, keep the second + keep first, differentiate second.

2. Quotient Rule

\( \left(\frac{u}{v}\right)' = \frac{vu' - uv'}{v^2} \)

"Low d-high minus high d-low over the square of what's below."

3. Chain Rule

\( \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy}{du} \cdot \frac{du}{dx} \)

Differentiate outer function, then multiply by derivative of inside.

4. Symmetry Rules

Even: \( f(-x) = f(x) \)
Odd: \( f(-x) = -f(x) \)

Even = y-axis reflection symmetry.
Odd = origin rotation symmetry.
