Understand what each formula calculates and the meaning of every variable.

1 of 6
Arithmetic Term Value

Arithmetic Term ($n^{\text{th}}$ Term)

$$T_n = a + (n - 1)d$$

What it does: Finds any term in a sequence where consecutive terms differ by adding or subtracting the exact same constant value.

• $T_n$: Value of the $n$-th term

• $a$: First term ($T_1$)

• $n$: Position / term number ($n \in \{1, 2, 3, \dots\}$)

• $d$: Common difference, where $d = T_2 - T_1 = T_n - T_{n-1}$

Geometric Term Value

Geometric Term ($n^{\text{th}}$ Term)

$$T_n = a r^{n - 1}$$

What it does: Finds any term in a sequence where consecutive terms are formed by multiplying by the exact same constant ratio.

• $T_n$: Value of the $n$-th term

• $a$: First term ($T_1$)

• $r$: Common ratio, where $r = \frac{T_2}{T_1} = \frac{T_n}{T_{n-1}}$

• $n$: Position / term number

Arithmetic Series Sum

Arithmetic Sum ($S_n$)

$$S_n = \frac{n}{2}[2a + (n - 1)d] = \frac{n}{2}(a + l)$$

What it does: Computes the total sum of the first $n$ arithmetic terms added together ($T_1 + T_2 + \dots + T_n$).

• $S_n$: Sum of the first $n$ terms

• $n$: How many terms are being summed

• $a$: First term  |  $d$: Common difference

• $l$: Last term ($l = T_n$). Use $\frac{n}{2}(a + l)$ when the last term is known!

Geometric Series Sum

Geometric Sum ($S_n$)

$$S_n = \frac{a(1 - r^n)}{1 - r} = \frac{a(r^n - 1)}{r - 1}, \quad r \neq 1$$

What it does: Computes the sum of the first $n$ terms in a geometric series.

• $S_n$: Sum of the first $n$ terms

• $a$: First term

• $r$: Common ratio ($r \neq 1$ because division by 0 is undefined)

• Tip: Both versions give the exact same answer!

💡 Pro-Tip for HSC Reference Sheet: Always remember that arithmetic sequences can grow forever without bound, but geometric sequences can converge to a single steady value if each step gets smaller ($|r| < 1$). That is why the Limiting Sum lives under the Geometric formulas!
Geometric Infinite Series

Limiting Sum (Sum to Infinity)

$$S = \frac{a}{1 - r}, \quad |r| < 1$$

What it does: Finds the finite numerical sum of infinitely many terms added forever.

• $S$: The limiting sum ($S_{\infty}$)

• $a$: First term

• $|r| < 1$: Crucial Condition! This series converges only when $-1 < r < 1$. If $|r| \ge 1$, the sum is infinite / diverges.

ARITHMETIC TERM $T_n = a + (n-1)d$

Example 1

Find the $10^{\text{th}}$ term of the sequence: $4, 7, 10, 13, \dots$

Step 1: Identify values

$a = 4$

$d = 7 - 4 = 3$

$n = 10$

Step 2: Substitute into formula

$T_{10} = 4 + (10 - 1)(3) = 4 + (9)(3) = 4 + 27 = \mathbf{31}$

GEOMETRIC TERM $T_n = ar^{n-1}$

Example 2

Find the $6^{\text{th}}$ term of the sequence: $3, 6, 12, 24, \dots$

Step 1: Identify values

$a = 3$

$r = \frac{6}{3} = 2$

$n = 6$

Step 2: Substitute into formula

$T_6 = 3 \times 2^{6 - 1} = 3 \times 2^5 = 3 \times 32 = \mathbf{96}$

ARITHMETIC SUM $S_n = \frac{n}{2}[2a+(n-1)d]$

Example 3

Find the sum of the first $12$ terms of: $2 + 5 + 8 + 11 + \dots$

Step 1: Identify values

$a = 2, \quad d = 5 - 2 = 3, \quad n = 12$

Step 2: Substitute into formula

$S_{12} = \frac{12}{2}\,[2(2) + (12 - 1)(3)]$

$S_{12} = 6 \times [4 + (11)(3)] = 6 \times [4 + 33] = 6 \times 37 = \mathbf{222}$

GEOMETRIC SUM $S_n = \frac{a(r^n - 1)}{r - 1}$

Example 4

Find the sum of the first $5$ terms of: $2 + 6 + 18 + 54 + \dots$

Step 1: Identify values

$a = 2, \quad r = \frac{6}{2} = 3, \quad n = 5$

Step 2: Substitute into formula

$S_5 = \frac{2(3^5 - 1)}{3 - 1} = \frac{2(243 - 1)}{2} = 243 - 1 = \mathbf{242}$

Notice: In Example 5, each term is halved ($r = 0.5$). Since $|0.5| < 1$, the terms get microscopically small and the sum never escapes $32$.

LIMITING SUM $S = \frac{a}{1 - r}$

Example 5

Find the limiting sum of: $16 + 8 + 4 + 2 + \dots$

Step 1: Check conditions

$a = 16, \quad r = \frac{8}{16} = \frac{1}{2} = 0.5$

Since $|r| = 0.5 < 1$, the limiting sum exists!

Step 2: Calculate sum

$S = \frac{16}{1 - 0.5} = \frac{16}{0.5} = \mathbf{32}$

ARITHMETIC TERM

Question 1

Given the sequence: $9, 14, 19, 24, \dots$

  • State $a$ and $d$.
  • Find the $15^{\text{th}}$ term ($T_{15}$).

$a = 9, \quad d = 14 - 9 = 5$

$T_{15} = 9 + (15 - 1)(5) = 9 + (14)(5) = 9 + 70 = \mathbf{79}$

GEOMETRIC TERM

Question 2

Given the sequence: $5, 15, 45, 135, \dots$

  • State $a$ and $r$.
  • Find the $7^{\text{th}}$ term ($T_{7}$).

$a = 5, \quad r = \frac{15}{5} = 3$

$T_7 = 5 \times 3^{7 - 1} = 5 \times 3^6 = 5 \times 729 = \mathbf{3645}$

ARITHMETIC SUM

Question 3

Calculate the sum of the first $20$ terms of the series:

$$1 + 5 + 9 + 13 + \dots$$

$a = 1, \quad d = 4, \quad n = 20$

$S_{20} = \frac{20}{2}[2(1) + (20 - 1)(4)] = 10 \times [2 + (19)(4)] = 10 \times [2 + 76] = 10 \times 78 = \mathbf{780}$

GEOMETRIC SUM

Question 4

Calculate the sum of the first $6$ terms of the series:

$$4 + 8 + 16 + 32 + \dots$$

$a = 4, \quad r = \frac{8}{4} = 2, \quad n = 6$

$S_6 = \frac{4(2^6 - 1)}{2 - 1} = 4(64 - 1) = 4 \times 63 = \mathbf{252}$

Remember: When checking whether a series has a limiting sum, $r$ can be negative or positive, but its size $|r|$ must remain strictly under $1$.
LIMITING SUM

Question 5

Find the limiting sum of the series:

$$54 + 18 + 6 + 2 + \dots$$

$a = 54, \quad r = \frac{18}{54} = \frac{1}{3}$

Since $\left|\frac{1}{3}\right| < 1$, sum exists.

$S = \frac{54}{1 - \frac{1}{3}} = \frac{54}{\frac{2}{3}} = 54 \times \frac{3}{2} = \mathbf{81}$

Question 1

Given the arithmetic sequence: $2, 6, 10, 14, \dots$

Find the $8^{\text{th}}$ term ($T_{8}$) using $T_n = a + (n-1)d$.

$a = 2, \quad d = 6 - 2 = 4, \quad n = 8$

$T_{8} = 2 + (8 - 1)(4) = 2 + (7)(4) = 2 + 28 = \mathbf{30}$

GEOMETRIC TERM

Question 2

Given the geometric sequence: $2, 4, 8, 16, \dots$

Find the $6^{\text{th}}$ term ($T_{6}$) using $T_n = ar^{n-1}$.

$a = 2, \quad r = \frac{4}{2} = 2, \quad n = 6$

$T_6 = 2 \times 2^{6 - 1} = 2 \times 2^5 = 2 \times 32 = \mathbf{64}$

ARITHMETIC SUM

Question 3

Find the sum of the first $10$ terms of:

$$3 + 6 + 9 + 12 + \dots$$

$a = 3, \quad d = 3, \quad n = 10$

$S_{10} = \frac{10}{2}[2(3) + (10 - 1)(3)] = 5 \times [6 + 27] = 5 \times 33 = \mathbf{165}$

GEOMETRIC SUM

Question 4

Find the sum of the first $4$ terms of:

$$3 + 6 + 12 + 24 + \dots$$

$a = 3, \quad r = 2, \quad n = 4$

$S_4 = \frac{3(2^4 - 1)}{2 - 1} = \frac{3(16 - 1)}{1} = 3 \times 15 = \mathbf{45}$

(Check: 3 + 6 + 12 + 24 = 45 ✓)

Notice how dividing each term by 2 means $r = \frac{1}{2} = 0.5$. Because $0.5 < 1$, the sum is finite and clean!
LIMITING SUM

Question 5

Find the limiting sum of:

$$40 + 20 + 10 + 5 + \dots$$

$a = 40, \quad r = \frac{20}{40} = 0.5$

Since $|r| = 0.5 < 1$, the limiting sum exists.

$S = \frac{40}{1 - 0.5} = \frac{40}{0.5} = \mathbf{80}$

ARITHMETIC TERM

GEOMETRIC TERM

Question 2

Given the geometric sequence: $1, 3, 9, 27, \dots$

Find the $5^{\text{th}}$ term ($T_5$).

$a = 1, \quad r = \frac{3}{1} = 3, \quad n = 5$

$T_5 = 1 \times 3^{5 - 1} = 3^4 = \mathbf{81}$

ARITHMETIC SUM

Question 3

Calculate the sum of the first $8$ terms of:

$$7 + 10 + 13 + 16 + \dots$$

$a = 7, \quad d = 10 - 7 = 3, \quad n = 8$

$S_8 = \frac{8}{2}[2(7) + (8 - 1)(3)] = 4 \times [14 + (7)(3)] = 4 \times [14 + 21] = 4 \times 35 = \mathbf{140}$

GEOMETRIC SUM

Question 4

Calculate the sum of the first $5$ terms of:

$$1 + 2 + 4 + 8 + \dots$$

$a = 1, \quad r = 2, \quad n = 5$

$S_5 = \frac{1(2^5 - 1)}{2 - 1} = \frac{32 - 1}{1} = \mathbf{31}$

(Check: 1 + 2 + 4 + 8 + 16 = 31 ✓)

Remember: When $r = \frac{1}{3}$, $1 - r = \frac{2}{3}$. Dividing by $\frac{2}{3}$ is the same as multiplying by $\frac{3}{2}$!
LIMITING SUM

Question 5

Find the limiting sum of:

$$18 + 6 + 2 + \frac{2}{3} + \dots$$

$a = 18, \quad r = \frac{6}{18} = \frac{1}{3}$

Since $\left|\frac{1}{3}\right| < 1$, the sum exists.

$S = \frac{18}{1 - \frac{1}{3}} = \frac{18}{\frac{2}{3}} = 18 \times \frac{3}{2} = \mathbf{27}$

ARITHMETIC TERM CHALLENGE

Challenge 1: Pocket Money Growth

Liam gets $\$10$ in Week 1, and his parents increase it by $\$3$ each week ($10, 13, 16, \dots$).

How much will he receive in Week 8?

Identify values: $a = 10, \quad d = 3, \quad n = 8$

$T_8 = 10 + (8 - 1)(3) = 10 + (7)(3) = 10 + 21 = \mathbf{\$31}$

GEOMETRIC TERM CHALLENGE

Challenge 2: Doubling Colony

A science experiment starts with $4$ cells. The count doubles every hour ($4, 8, 16, \dots$).

How many cells are there in the $5^{\text{th}}$ hour?

Identify values: $a = 4, \quad r = 2, \quad n = 5$

$T_5 = 4 \times 2^{5 - 1} = 4 \times 2^4 = 4 \times 16 = \mathbf{64\text{ cells}}$

ARITHMETIC SUM CHALLENGE

Challenge 3: Daily Reading

Chloe reads $5$ pages on Day 1, $7$ on Day 2, $9$ on Day 3, and continues increasing by $2$ pages daily.

How many total pages does she read in $6$ days?

Identify values: $a = 5, \quad d = 2, \quad n = 6$

$S_6 = \frac{6}{2}[2(5) + (6 - 1)(2)] = 3 \times [10 + (5)(2)] = 3 \times 20 = \mathbf{60\text{ pages}}$

GEOMETRIC SUM CHALLENGE

Challenge 4: Fitness Step Bonus

A fitness app awards $100$ bonus points on Day 1, and triples the bonus each day ($100, 300, 900$).

What is the total sum of points over the first $3$ days?

Identify values: $a = 100, \quad r = 3, \quad n = 3$

$S_3 = \frac{100(3^3 - 1)}{3 - 1} = \frac{100(26)}{2} = \mathbf{1300\text{ points}}$

LIMITING SUM CHALLENGE

Challenge 5: Bouncing Ball Drop

A rubber ball is dropped from a height of $10\text{ m}$. Each bounce is half as high ($10 + 5 + 2.5 + \dots$).

Find the total downward distance dropped.

Identify values: $a = 10, \quad r = 0.5$

Since $|0.5| < 1$, the sum exists.

$S = \frac{10}{1 - 0.5} = \frac{10}{0.5} = \mathbf{20\text{ metres}}$
