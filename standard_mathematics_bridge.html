Missed Topic 1

Financial Mathematics: Taxation, Pay & Allowances

Advanced does not study the Australian progressive income tax system or holiday loading.

High HSC Weighting
1. Taxable Income, Medicare & Tax Payable
  • Taxable Income: $$\text{Taxable Income} = \text{Gross Income} - \text{Allowable Deductions}$$ Allowable deductions include work-related gear, uniform expenses, donations $> \$2$, and union fees.
  • Medicare Levy: Usually a flat $2\%$ of your Taxable Income: $$\text{Medicare Levy} = 0.02 \times \text{Taxable Income}$$
  • Total Tax Owed: $$\text{Total Tax Owed} = \text{Gross Tax (from table)} + \text{Medicare Levy} - \text{Tax Offsets}$$
  • Tax Refund vs Tax Debt: Compare what you owe against the tax your employer withheld throughout the year (PAYG):
    • If $\text{Tax Withheld} > \text{Total Tax Owed} \implies \textbf{Tax Refund}$
    • If $\text{Tax Withheld} < \text{Total Tax Owed} \implies \textbf{Tax Debt (Payable)}$
2. Holiday Leave Loading & Earning Types
  • Annual Leave Loading: In Australia, workers receive an extra $17.5\%$ bonus on 4 weeks of standard pay when taking annual leave: $$\text{Leave Loading} = 17.5\% \times (4 \times \text{Normal Weekly Pay}) = 0.175 \times 4 \times W$$ $$\text{Total Holiday Pay} = (4 \times \text{Normal Weekly Pay}) + \text{Leave Loading}$$
  • Overtime Rates:
    • Time-and-a-half: $\text{Hourly Rate} \times 1.5$
    • Double time: $\text{Hourly Rate} \times 2.0$
  • Piecework & Commission: Paid per unit produced ($\text{Rate} \times n$) or as a percentage of total sales made.
  • Royalties: Payment based on a percentage of the retail price of books, music, or patents sold.

Missed Topic 2

Measurement: Limits of Accuracy & The Trapezoidal Rule

Every measurement is an approximation. Standard examines precision, errors, and approximate area formulas.

Exclusive to Standard
1. Precision, Absolute Error & Bounds
  • Precision (Smallest Unit): The smallest division on the measuring tool (e.g., a ruler marked in millimeters has a precision of $1\text{ mm}$).
  • Absolute Error: Half of the precision: $$\text{Absolute Error} = \pm \frac{1}{2} \times \text{Precision}$$ Example: A reading of $24\text{ cm}$ measured to the nearest $\text{cm}$ has an absolute error of $\pm 0.5\text{ cm}$.
  • Limits of Accuracy (Lower & Upper Bounds): $$\text{Lower Bound} = \text{Measurement} - \text{Absolute Error}$$ $$\text{Upper Bound} = \text{Measurement} + \text{Absolute Error}$$
  • Percentage Error: $$\text{Percentage Error} = \frac{\text{Absolute Error}}{\text{Measurement}} \times 100\%$$
2. The Trapezoidal Rule (Approximating Area)
  • In Advanced, you use definite integrals $\int_a^b f(x)dx$. In Standard, irregular regions (such as rivers or estates) are approximated using trapeziums:
  • Single Application: $$A \approx \frac{h}{2}(d_f + d_l)$$ Where $h$ is the distance between offsets, $d_f$ is the first offset (first measurement), and $d_l$ is the last offset.
  • Multiple Applications (Two Trapezia): $$A \approx \frac{h}{2}(d_f + d_m) + \frac{h}{2}(d_m + d_l) = \frac{h}{2}(d_f + 2d_m + d_l)$$ Where $d_m$ is the middle offset.
  • Boundary Note: If the river or plot tapers to a point at an end, that offset is $0$!
Missed Topic 3

Data Analysis: Five-Number Summary & Formal Outlier Fences

Advanced introduces statistics informally; Standard requires strict algebraic tests for outliers and parallel box plot comparisons.

Guaranteed HSC Mark
1. The Formal Outlier Fence Formula
  • A score is officially classified as an outlier if it lies outside the lower and upper fences:
  • $$\text{Lower Fence} = Q_1 - 1.5 \times \text{IQR}$$ $$\text{Upper Fence} = Q_3 + 1.5 \times \text{IQR}$$
  • Where $\text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1$ (Interquartile Range).
  • On a Box Plot: Any outlier is indicated by a separate individual dot or cross ($\times$), and the whisker only extends to the next non-outlier value.
2. Comparing Parallel Box Plots in Words
  • HSC markers award full marks for comparing distributions across three pillars with numerical backing:
  • 1. Centre (Median): State which group scored higher on average using the medians (e.g., "Group A had a higher median of 72 compared to Group B's 61").
  • 2. Spread (IQR & Range): State which group was more consistent (smaller $\text{IQR} = \text{more consistent}$).
  • 3. Shape & Skewness:
    • $\text{Symmetrical}$: Whiskers and boxes roughly equal.
    • $\text{Positively Skewed}$: Long tail/whisker to the right.
    • $\text{Negatively Skewed}$: Long tail/whisker to the left.
Missed Topic 4

Depreciation & Flat-Rate Loans

How assets lose value over time and how non-reducing loans charge interest.

Year 12 Foundation
1. Straight-Line vs Declining-Balance Depreciation
  • Straight-Line Depreciation: The asset loses the exact same dollar amount $D$ every time period: $$S = V_0 - Dn$$ Where $S$ is salvage value, $V_0$ is initial cost, and $D = V_0 \times r$.
  • Declining-Balance Depreciation: The asset loses a percentage $r$ of its remaining value each year (compound decay): $$S = V_0(1 - r)^n$$
  • Notice that declining-balance never reaches absolute zero, whereas straight-line eventually hits zero salvage value.
2. Flat-Rate Personal Loans & Repayments
  • In a flat-rate loan, simple interest is charged on the original principal $P$ for the entire duration, regardless of repayments made: $$I = Prn$$
  • Total Amount to Repay: $$A = P + I = P + Prn$$
  • Repayment Calculation: $$\text{Periodic Repayment} = \frac{A}{\text{Total Number of Repayments}}$$ (e.g., for a 4-year monthly loan, divide by $4 \times 12 = 48$).

Q1 • Taxation & Medicare 4 Marks

Calculating Tax Payable & Refund

Liam earns a gross annual salary of $\$84,000$. He claims $\$1,500$ in work-related travel and $\$500$ in union fees as allowable deductions.

(a) Calculate Liam's taxable income.
(b) Using the tax table above ($45,001–$120,000 bracket: $\$5,092$ plus $32.5\text{c}$ for each $\$1$ over $\$45,000$), calculate his gross tax payable.
(c) If the Medicare levy is $2\%$ of taxable income, and Liam's employer has deducted $\$19,200$ in PAYG tax, determine if he receives a tax refund or owes tax, and calculate the amount.

Taxable income = Gross - Deductions. Add the 2% Medicare levy to the gross tax to find Total Tax Owed, then compare with PAYG tax paid ($19,200).

Worked Solution:

(a) Taxable Income:
$\text{Taxable Income} = 84000 - (1500 + 500) = \$82,000$.

(b) Gross Tax:
$\text{Excess over } 45000 = 82000 - 45000 = \$37,000$.
$\text{Tax} = 5092 + (0.325 \times 37000) = 5092 + 12025 = \$17,117$.

(c) Medicare & Refund / Debt:
$\text{Medicare Levy} = 0.02 \times 82000 = \$1,640$.
$\text{Total Tax Owed} = 17117 + 1640 = \$18,757$.
Since Liam paid $\$19,200$ (which is greater than $\$18,757$), he receives a Tax Refund:
$\text{Refund} = 19200 - 18757 = \mathbf{\$443}$.

Q2 • Holiday Leave Loading 3 Marks

Four Weeks Annual Leave Calculation

Zoe is paid a regular wage of $\$28.50$ per hour for a $38$-hour working week. She takes $4$ weeks of annual leave and is entitled to the standard $17.5\%$ annual leave loading.

Calculate the total holiday pay Zoe will receive for her $4$-week vacation.

Find 1 week's wage ($28.50 \times 38$), then multiply by 4 for 4 weeks. Calculate 17.5% loading on this 4-week amount and add them together (or multiply 4 weeks by 1.175).

Worked Solution:

1. Normal weekly wage = $28.50 \times 38 = \$1,083$.

2. Normal pay for 4 weeks = $4 \times 1083 = \$4,332$.

3. Leave loading ($17.5\%$) = $0.175 \times 4332 = \$758.10$.

$$\text{Total Holiday Pay} = 4332 + 758.10 = \mathbf{\$5,090.10}$$

Q3 • Limits of Accuracy 3 Marks

Upper & Lower Bounds on Perimeter

A rectangular room has its length measured as $8\text{ m}$ and its width measured as $5\text{ m}$, both to the nearest metre.

(a) State the absolute error of these measurements.
(b) Calculate the minimum (lower bound) possible perimeter of the room.
(c) Calculate the maximum (upper bound) possible area of the room.

The precision is 1 m. Absolute error is half of this: $\pm 0.5\text{ m}$. For min perimeter, use the lower bounds of length and width. For max area, multiply the upper bounds.

Worked Solution:

(a) Absolute Error: $\pm \frac{1}{2} \times 1\text{ m} = \pm 0.5\text{ m}$.

(b) Lower Bound Perimeter:
Length lower bound = $8 - 0.5 = 7.5\text{ m}$.
Width lower bound = $5 - 0.5 = 4.5\text{ m}$.
$\text{Min Perimeter} = 2(7.5 + 4.5) = 2(12) = \mathbf{24\text{ m}}$.

(c) Upper Bound Area:
Length upper bound = $8.5\text{ m}$; Width upper bound = $5.5\text{ m}$.
$\text{Max Area} = 8.5 \times 5.5 = \mathbf{46.75\text{ m}^2}$.

Q4 • Percentage Error 2 Marks

Precision & Relative Accuracy

A piece of copper wire is measured using digital calipers as $12.4\text{ mm}$ (measured to the nearest tenth of a millimetre).

Find the percentage error of this measurement, correct to two decimal places.

Nearest tenth = $0.1\text{ mm}$. Absolute error = half of $0.1\text{ mm} = 0.05\text{ mm}$. Then divide by 12.4 and multiply by 100%.

Worked Solution:

1. Precision = $0.1\text{ mm}$.

2. Absolute error = $\pm \frac{0.1}{2} = 0.05\text{ mm}$.

$$\text{Percentage Error} = \frac{0.05}{12.4} \times 100\% \approx \mathbf{0.40\%}$$

Q5 • Trapezoidal Rule 3 Marks

Two-Application Area Approximation

A council park borders a straight road and an irregular river. Measurements across the park are taken at equal intervals of $15\text{ m}$.

The three parallel offset measurements perpendicular to the road are $18\text{ m}$, $26\text{ m}$, and $14\text{ m}$.
Use two applications of the trapezoidal rule to estimate the total surface area of the park.

Apply $A \approx \frac{h}{2}(d_f + 2d_m + d_l)$ where $h = 15\text{ m}$, $d_f = 18$, $d_m = 26$, and $d_l = 14$.

Worked Solution:

$$A_1 = \frac{15}{2}(18 + 26) = 7.5(44) = 330\text{ m}^2$$

$$A_2 = \frac{15}{2}(26 + 14) = 7.5(40) = 300\text{ m}^2$$

$$\text{Total Area} = 330 + 300 = \mathbf{630\text{ m}^2}$$

(Or via formula: $A \approx \frac{15}{2}(18 + 2(26) + 14) = 7.5(84) = 630\text{ m}^2$.)

Q6 • Outlier Fences 3 Marks

Formal Algebraic Test for Outliers

The test marks for a class of students have a five-number summary of:

$\text{Min} = 34, \quad Q_1 = 52, \quad \text{Median} = 66, \quad Q_3 = 74, \quad \text{Max} = 98$

Determine mathematically whether the score of $34$ is an outlier. Show full working using the outlier rule.

Calculate $\text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1$. Then find the lower fence $= Q_1 - 1.5 \times \text{IQR}$. If $34$ is less than this boundary, it is an outlier.

Worked Solution:

1. $\text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1 = 74 - 52 = 22$.

2. $\text{Lower Fence} = Q_1 - 1.5 \times \text{IQR} = 52 - (1.5 \times 22) = 52 - 33 = 19$.

3. Comparing score: Since $34 > 19$, the score of $34$ is above the lower threshold.

$$\therefore 34 \text{ is NOT an outlier.}$$

Q7 • Comparing Box Plots 3 Marks

HSC Criteria Writing: Centre, Spread & Shape

Two athletes recorded their running times over 20 training sprints:

  • Runner A: $\text{Median} = 11.2\text{ s}, \quad \text{IQR} = 0.4\text{ s}$, symmetrical distribution.
  • Runner B: $\text{Median} = 11.6\text{ s}, \quad \text{IQR} = 1.1\text{ s}$, positively skewed.

Compare the performance of the two runners with reference to both centre and spread. State who is more consistent.

For sprint times, lower is faster! Runner A has a lower median. Consistency relates to smaller IQR.

Worked Solution (Model HSC Response):

1. Centre (Speed): Runner A was generally faster on average, recording a lower median sprint time of $11.2\text{ s}$ compared to Runner B's median of $11.6\text{ s}$.

2. Spread (Consistency): Runner A was significantly more consistent because their interquartile range ($\text{IQR} = 0.4\text{ s}$) is much smaller than Runner B's ($\text{IQR} = 1.1\text{ s}$).

Q8 • Depreciation Comparison 4 Marks

Straight-Line vs Declining-Balance

A company purchases a machine for $\$40,000$.

(a) If the machine depreciates by straight-line method at $\$4,500$ per year, calculate its salvage value after $5$ years.
(b) If an alternative machine costs $\$40,000$ and depreciates using declining-balance at a rate of $15\%$ p.a., find its salvage value after $5$ years (nearest dollar).

For straight-line: $S = V_0 - Dn$. For declining-balance: $S = V_0(1 - r)^n$.

Worked Solution:

(a) Straight-Line:
$$S = 40000 - (4500 \times 5) = 40000 - 22500 = \mathbf{\$17,500}$$

(b) Declining-Balance:
$$S = 40000(1 - 0.15)^5 = 40000(0.85)^5 \approx \mathbf{\$17,748}$$

Q9 • Flat-Rate Personal Loan 3 Marks

Simple Interest Total Repayment

Maya borrows $\$12,000$ to buy a used car with a flat-rate personal loan over $3$ years at a simple interest rate of $8.5\%$ p.a.

(a) Calculate the total interest charged on the loan.
(b) Calculate the monthly repayment Maya must make.

Interest $I = Prn$. Total to repay $A = P + I$. Then divide by the total number of months ($3 \times 12 = 36$).

Worked Solution:

(a) Interest:
$$I = 12000 \times 0.085 \times 3 = \mathbf{\$3,060}$$

(b) Monthly Repayment:
Total to repay $A = 12000 + 3060 = \$15,060$.
Total months = $3 \times 12 = 36$.
$$\text{Monthly Repayment} = \frac{15060}{36} = \mathbf{\$418.33}$$
