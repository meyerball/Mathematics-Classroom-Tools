Module 1

High-Yield Theory & Formula Reference

Review these core definitions and algebraic relationships. You will apply them continuously across Year 12.

1. Differential Calculus Core Rules
  • Product Rule: $\frac{d}{dx}[uv] = u'v + uv'$. Use whenever two variable terms are multiplied (e.g., $x^2 \sqrt{x+1}$).
  • Quotient Rule: $\frac{d}{dx}\left[\frac{u}{v}\right] = \frac{v u' - u v'}{v^2}$. Mnemonic: "Low d-high minus high d-low over the square of what's below."
  • Chain Rule (Composite Functions): $\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{dy}{du} \cdot \frac{du}{dx}$, or $\frac{d}{dx}[f(g(x))] = f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x)$. Differentiate the outer function, then multiply by the derivative of the inside.
  • Tangents & Normals: Gradient of tangent is $m_T = f'(x_0)$. Normal is perpendicular: $m_N = -\frac{1}{m_T}$. Point-gradient equation: $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$.
2. Trigonometry & The Unit Circle
  • Pythagorean Identities: $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$, $\tan\theta = \frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}$, $1 + \tan^2\theta = \sec^2\theta$.
  • ASTC Quadrant Rule: Quadrant I (All $+$), Quadrant II ($\sin +$), Quadrant III ($\tan +$), Quadrant IV ($\cos +$).
  • Reference Angles ($\theta_{\text{ref}}$): Q2: $\pi - \theta_{\text{ref}}$, Q3: $\pi + \theta_{\text{ref}}$, Q4: $2\pi - \theta_{\text{ref}}$.
  • Exact Values: $\frac{\pi}{6} = 30^\circ$, $\frac{\pi}{4} = 45^\circ$, $\frac{\pi}{3} = 60^\circ$, $\frac{\pi}{2} = 90^\circ$.
3. Functions, Symmetries & Domains
  • Even Function: $f(-x) = f(x)$ for all $x$ in domain. Geometrically symmetric across the $y$-axis (line of reflection $x=0$).
  • Odd Function: $f(-x) = -f(x)$ for all $x$ in domain. Possesses point symmetry ($180^\circ$ rotational symmetry about $(0,0)$).
  • Domain Restrictions: Denominators $\ne 0$; Square roots $\sqrt{f(x)} \implies f(x) \ge 0$; Square roots in denominator $\frac{1}{\sqrt{f(x)}} \implies f(x) > 0$.
  • Graph Shifts: $f(x - c)$ shifts right by $c$; $f(x + c)$ shifts left by $c$; $f(x) + c$ shifts up by $c$.
4. Probability & Tree Diagrams
  • Without Replacement: Total sample size decreases by 1 on each subsequent branch (denominator shrinks).
  • Complementary Events: $P(\text{at least one}) = 1 - P(\text{none})$. Saves time in multi-branch calculations.
  • Conditional Probability: $P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}$. Restrict sample space strictly to condition $B$.
  • Multiplication along branches: Multiply probabilities along paths; add disjoint branch results together.
Module 2

HSC Marker Pitfall Clinic: The 3 Costliest Traps

These three specific errors account for the majority of lost marks in Year 11 and Year 12 assessment tasks.

Trap 1: The Minus Sign in Quotient Rule

Common Student Mistake:

$f'(x) = \frac{(x^2+1)(2) - 4x^2 - 2x}{(x^2+1)^2}$

Forgetting parentheses around $u v' = (2x - 1)(2x) = 4x^2 - 2x$. The negative sign must distribute to both terms!

The Fix (Mandatory Brackets):

$-[4x^2 - 2x] = -4x^2 + 2x$

Always write square brackets around the second term of the quotient rule numerator before expanding.

Takeaway: Swapping $u v'$ and $v u'$ in the quotient rule flips the signs completely and leads to zero marks for the derivative.
Trap 2: Ignoring Domain Boundaries

Common Student Mistake:

$\cos\theta = 1 \implies \theta = 0$ (Only!)

Remembering $0$, but missing $2\pi$. If the question specifies $0 \le \theta \le 2\pi$, both endpoints are valid and required!

The Fix (Domain Checklist):

Check inequality symbols: if $\le$ is used, evaluate the boundary values immediately before solving for internal quadrants.

Takeaway: If the domain is $0 \le \theta < 2\pi$, including $2\pi$ is an extraneous root. Always read the inequality signs carefully.
Trap 3: Strict Inequality in Denominators

Common Student Mistake:

Domain: $-4 \le x \le 4$

Writing $\ge 0$ out of habit for square roots. But when the root is in the denominator, zero creates an undefined division.

The Fix (Strict Inequality):

$\sqrt{f(x)}$ in denom $\implies f(x) > 0$ strictly!

Round brackets $(-4, 4)$ instead of square brackets $[-4, 4]$.

Takeaway: Check your brackets. Open interval $(-4, 4)$ means vertical asymptotes exist at $x = \pm 4$.
Module 3 Transition Bridge

The Gateway to Year 12: How Year 11 Connects

Year 12 builds directly on Year 11 skills. Here is how your current revision connects immediately to Term 1 of Year 12.

Differential → Integral Calculus

In Year 11, you found the gradient of a curve using $\frac{dy}{dx}$. In Year 12, you will reverse this process through Integration:

$$\int 2x \, dx = x^2 + C$$

To calculate areas bounded between curves and volumes of revolution, standard differentiation rules must be second nature.

Trigonometry → Trig Calculus

In Year 12 Term 1, we differentiate and integrate trigonometric functions:

$$\frac{d}{dx}[\sin(x)] = \cos(x)$$

Finding stationary points and curve sketching requires solving equations like $\cos(2x) = 0$ across multiple quadrants quickly.

Functions → Curve Sketching & Inflection

Year 12 introduces the Second Derivative $f''(x)$:

$$f''(x) > 0 \implies \text{Concave Up}$$

Classifying maximums, minimums, horizontal and oblique points of inflection combines polynomial algebra, symmetry, and calculus into one problem.

Q1 • Product Rule 3 Marks

Differentiating a Product with Surds

Differentiate $y = (3x - 2)\sqrt{x+1}$ with respect to $x$. Express your final answer as a single simplified algebraic fraction.

Let $u = 3x - 2$ and $v = (x+1)^{1/2}$. Apply $(uv)' = u'v + uv'$, then bring to a common denominator of $2\sqrt{x+1}$.

Worked Solution:

$u = 3x - 2 \implies u' = 3$

$v = (x+1)^{1/2} \implies v' = \frac{1}{2}(x+1)^{-1/2} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x+1}}$

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = 3\sqrt{x+1} + \frac{3x - 2}{2\sqrt{x+1}}$$

Common denominator:

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{6(x+1) + (3x - 2)}{2\sqrt{x+1}} = \frac{9x + 4}{2\sqrt{x+1}}$$

Q2 • Quotient Rule 3 Marks

Rational Function & Normal Gradient

Find the gradient of the normal to the curve $y = \frac{x^2 - 3}{2x + 1}$ at the point where $x = 1$.

Find $y' = \frac{v u' - u v'}{v^2}$. Substitute $x = 1$ to get the tangent gradient $m_T$. The normal gradient is $m_N = -\frac{1}{m_T}$.

Worked Solution:

$u = x^2 - 3 \implies u' = 2x$

$v = 2x + 1 \implies v' = 2$

$$y' = \frac{(2x+1)(2x) - (x^2-3)(2)}{(2x+1)^2} = \frac{4x^2 + 2x - 2x^2 + 6}{(2x+1)^2} = \frac{2x^2 + 2x + 6}{(2x+1)^2}$$

At $x = 1$: $m_T = \frac{2(1)^2 + 2(1) + 6}{(2(1)+1)^2} = \frac{10}{9}$.

$$\therefore m_{\text{normal}} = -\frac{1}{m_T} = -\frac{9}{10}$$

Q3 • Chain Rule 3 Marks

Repeated Composite Differentiation

Differentiate $f(x) = \frac{1}{(5 - 2x^3)^4}$ and determine all values of $x$ for which $f'(x) > 0$.

Rewrite as $f(x) = (5 - 2x^3)^{-4}$. Apply the chain rule. Then analyze the sign of the numerator and denominator.

Worked Solution:

$$f'(x) = -4(5 - 2x^3)^{-5} \cdot (-6x^2) = \frac{24x^2}{(5 - 2x^3)^5}$$

For $f'(x) > 0$: Since $24x^2 \ge 0$ for all real $x$ (with $24x^2 = 0$ at $x = 0$), the numerator is positive whenever $x \neq 0$.

The sign depends on the denominator: $(5 - 2x^3)^5 > 0 \iff 5 - 2x^3 > 0 \iff x^3 < \frac{5}{2} \iff x < \sqrt[3]{\frac{5}{2}}$.

$$\therefore x < \sqrt[3]{\frac{5}{2}}, \quad x \neq 0$$

Q4 • Symmetry 3 Marks

Algebraic Proof of Function Symmetry

Determine algebraically whether the function $h(x) = \frac{x^3 - 4x}{x^2 + 9}$ is even, odd, or neither. Describe its geometric symmetry.

Substitute $-x$ into $h(x)$ and compare with $h(x)$ and $-h(x)$. Remember $(-x)^3 = -x^3$ and $(-x)^2 = x^2$.

Worked Solution:

$$h(-x) = \frac{(-x)^3 - 4(-x)}{(-x)^2 + 9} = \frac{-x^3 + 4x}{x^2 + 9} = \frac{-(x^3 - 4x)}{x^2 + 9} = -h(x)$$

Since $h(-x) = -h(x)$ for all real $x$, $h(x)$ is an odd function.

Geometric feature: Possesses point symmetry ($180^\circ$ rotational symmetry) about the origin $(0,0)$.

Q5 • Trig Equations 3 Marks

Compound Angle Exact Solutions

Solve $2\sin\left(2\theta - \frac{\pi}{6}\right) = \sqrt{3}$ for $0 \le \theta \le \pi$. Give exact radian solutions.

Transform the domain: If $0 \le \theta \le \pi$, then $-\frac{\pi}{6} \le 2\theta - \frac{\pi}{6} \le \frac{11\pi}{6}$. Then find angles where $\sin = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$.

Worked Solution:

Let $\alpha = 2\theta - \frac{\pi}{6}$. Equation: $\sin\alpha = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$.

Domain: $-\frac{\pi}{6} \le \alpha \le \frac{11\pi}{6}$.

Reference angle is $\frac{\pi}{3}$. Sin is positive in Quadrants I and II:

$\alpha = \frac{\pi}{3}, \frac{2\pi}{3}$.

Solving for $\theta$:

1) $2\theta - \frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{\pi}{3} \implies 2\theta = \frac{3\pi}{6} = \frac{\pi}{2} \implies \theta = \frac{\pi}{4}$.

2) $2\theta - \frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{2\pi}{3} \implies 2\theta = \frac{5\pi}{6} \implies \theta = \frac{5\pi}{12}$.

$$\therefore \theta = \frac{\pi}{4}, \; \frac{5\pi}{12}$$

Q6 • Trig Identities 3 Marks

Pythagorean Identity Algebraic Proof

Prove the identity: $\frac{1 + \sin\theta}{\cos\theta} + \frac{\cos\theta}{1 + \sin\theta} = \frac{2}{\cos\theta}$.

Combine the LHS under a common denominator of $\cos\theta(1 + \sin\theta)$. Expand the numerator and apply $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$.

Worked Solution:

$$\text{LHS} = \frac{(1 + \sin\theta)^2 + \cos^2\theta}{\cos\theta(1 + \sin\theta)}$$

$$= \frac{1 + 2\sin\theta + \sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta}{\cos\theta(1 + \sin\theta)}$$

Substitute $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$:

$$= \frac{1 + 2\sin\theta + 1}{\cos\theta(1 + \sin\theta)} = \frac{2 + 2\sin\theta}{\cos\theta(1 + \sin\theta)} = \frac{2(1 + \sin\theta)}{\cos\theta(1 + \sin\theta)} = \frac{2}{\cos\theta} = \text{RHS}$$

Q7 • Function Notation 3 Marks

Composite Notation & Horizontal Translation

Given $f(x) = 2x^2 - 3x + 1$, expand and simplify $f(x - 2)$. Hence describe the geometric transformation mapping $y = f(x)$ to $y = f(x - 2)$.

Replace every $x$ with the entire binomial $(x - 2)$. Remember that $(x - 2)^2 = x^2 - 4x + 4$.

Worked Solution:

$$f(x - 2) = 2(x - 2)^2 - 3(x - 2) + 1$$

$$= 2(x^2 - 4x + 4) - 3x + 6 + 1 = 2x^2 - 8x + 8 - 3x + 7$$

$$= 2x^2 - 11x + 15$$

Geometric Transformation: A horizontal translation of 2 units to the right (positive $x$-direction).

Q8 • Piecewise Functions 3 Marks

Sub-domain Evaluation & Continuity Testing

A piecewise function is defined by:

$$f(x) = \begin{cases} 4 - x^2 & \text{for } x < 1 \\ 2x + 1 & \text{for } x \ge 1 \end{cases}$$

(a) Evaluate $f(-3)$ and $f(1)$.
(b) Determine whether $f(x)$ is continuous at $x = 1$.

Check which branch holds for each value of $x$. For continuity, check whether the left-hand limit $\lim_{x \to 1^-} f(x)$ matches the right-hand value $f(1)$.

Worked Solution:

Part (a):

  • $x = -3 < 1 \implies f(-3) = 4 - (-3)^2 = 4 - 9 = -5$.
  • $x = 1 \ge 1 \implies f(1) = 2(1) + 1 = 3$.

Part (b) Continuity test at $x = 1$:

Left branch as $x \to 1^-$: $4 - (1)^2 = 3$.

Right branch as $x \to 1^+$: $2(1) + 1 = 3$.

Since $\lim_{x \to 1^-} f(x) = \lim_{x \to 1^+} f(x) = f(1) = 3$, the function is continuous at $x = 1$.

Q9 • Probability Trees 3 Marks

Sampling Without Replacement & Conditionals

A box contains 5 red balls and 3 green balls. Two balls are drawn randomly without replacement.

(a) Find the probability that both balls are the same colour.
(b) Given that at least one green ball is selected, find the probability that both are green.

Total balls = 8. For second draw, denominator decreases to 7. For (b), use $P(A|B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}$ where $B = \text{at least one green} = 1 - P(RR)$.

Worked Solution:

Total balls = 8. First draw: $P(R) = \frac{5}{8}, P(G) = \frac{3}{8}$.

Part (a): Same colour:

$$P(\text{Same}) = P(RR) + P(GG) = \left(\frac{5}{8} \times \frac{4}{7}\right) + \left(\frac{3}{8} \times \frac{2}{7}\right) = \frac{20}{56} + \frac{6}{56} = \frac{26}{56} = \frac{13}{28}$$

Part (b): Conditional Probability:

$P(\text{At least 1 green}) = 1 - P(RR) = 1 - \frac{20}{56} = \frac{36}{56}$.

$$P(\text{Both } G \mid \text{At least 1 } G) = \frac{P(GG)}{P(\text{At least 1 } G)} = \frac{6/56}{36/56} = \frac{6}{36} = \frac{1}{6}$$
